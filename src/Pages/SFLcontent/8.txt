Relief went through the crew so strongly it was almost dizzying. They had a place. They had a purpose. For tonight, at least, they were safe.

"Excellent," said Guildmaster Ember-Smoke, and a slow smile spread across his old, rough face. "Every team needs a name. It is your banner, the call sign by which you will be known. Have you decided on one?"

"Team Sagittarius!" Kenji chirped, his loyalty unwavering.

"Absolutely not," Zax snarled, the name a fresh wound. "That name died with our crew. We will not dishonor them by carrying it as... this."

"But we should honor them," Anya countered, her voice thick with emotion. "We survived. We rose from the ashes. Team Phoenix?"

"A phoenix is reborn as it was," T'Vella stated, her logic a cold comfort. "We have not been reborn; we have been fundamentally altered. A name should reflect our current, illogical state."

Eva listened. The choice, as always, came to rest with her. They were children of the stars, now fallen to a strange new earth. "Team Starfall," she said, the name a perfect, tragic summary. "We are Team Starfall."

"Team Starfall," the Guildmaster repeated, nodding slowly. "A strong name. It speaks of a long journey and a new beginning." He gestured with his head towards a stack of fresh scrolls on a low table. "Then all that remains is the formality of registration."

Eva looked at the scroll. The script was a flowing, elegant series of pictographs and symbols that were completely alien to her. She couldn't read a single character. However, the layout was universal. There were clear lines for names, titles, and a larger space at the bottom, presumably for a signature or mark. It was a form, and she knew how to fill out a form, even if she couldn't read the fine print.

"T'Vella," Eva said, her voice low. "You're on scribe duty."

T'Vella stepped forward, her Gardevoir form moving with a quiet grace. She took the pencil he offered, a plain stick of wood with a blade-cut point like Leaf's, her slender fingers surprisingly adept. As Eva dictated the name, T'Vella carefully copied the sounds into the clean, angular letters of Federation Standard.

Ember-Smoke leaned forward, his old eyes studying the strange, efficient symbols. "A curious script," he commented mildly, "but I cannot read these markings. For the record, please state what you have written."

"Our apologies, Guildmaster," Eva said, gesturing to the scroll. "This is the script of our homeland. The first line is our team name: 'Team Starfall'."

"And the members?" he prompted.

Eva dictated their names and roles; T'Vella wrote each one, and Eva read it back for the record.

"My name, Eva. My role is Leader," Eva stated as T'Vella inscribed the line.

"Our Scholar, T'Vella."

"Our Guardian, Zax."

The process continued for each member—Crafter, Coordinator, Scout—until the scroll was filled with their strange, alien script, each line verbally confirmed for the Guild's official record.

Ember-Smoke nodded slowly, absorbing the information. "I have never seen its like," he mused, looking at the finished document. "So precise. From a very distant land, indeed." He showed no suspicion, only a deep, scholarly curiosity, but he did not press. Wisdom taught him that some stories unfold in their own time. Once the scroll was signed, he stamped it with a seal made from hardened sap, making it official. Team Starfall was born.

"You are dismissed," the Guildmaster said gently. "And unless I am very much mistaken, Cinder has not gone to bed either, and is waiting outside to show you to your quarters. Get some rest, Team Starfall. You have earned it."

The team gave a collective nod of gratitude and turned, opening the heavy wooden door. Cinder was waiting for them in the corridor, leaning against the curved wall of the great tree. He pushed himself upright as they emerged.

"He told me to go to bed," he said, before anyone could ask. "I will. Alright, follow me. The Guildmaster has arranged for one of the empty team rooms for you." He took a step, then paused, getting a good look at them in the crystal light of the hallway. He saw the soot still clinging to Anya's fur, the way Kenji was trembling on Zax's shoulder, the deep, bone-weary exhaustion in all their eyes. He sighed, changing his mind. "On second thought... you all look like you're about to collapse. The room can wait. Let's get some food in you first. Come on."

He led them down to the Guild's mess hall. At this hour it was nearly empty: a long, low room of tables and benches, lit by a few crystals turned low, with a hearth at the far end where a pot was being kept warm for the night shift. One table held a team just in from somewhere, three of them eating in tired silence. They looked up as Team Starfall came in, stared, and went back to their bowls. The air was warm and thick with the rich scent of roasted chestnuts, the earthy aroma of a simmering mushroom stew, and the sharp, sweet tang of freshly pressed apples. Cinder said a word to the cook behind the hearth, and bowls came. Team Starfall, a silent, soot-stained island, took a table in the corner, too exhausted to care who was looking. The food was a revelation. For the first time since the crash, they tasted something other than blue berries and fear. They ate in a deep, focused silence, the simple act of a hot, nourishing meal a more potent comfort than any words could offer.

After their meal, their bellies full and the worst of their fatigue abated, Cinder led them away from the main hall. They walked down a different corridor, one that seemed to spiral deeper into the base of the great tree. The air grew cooler and smelled of damp earth and moss. The team rooms, it seemed, were located in the more secluded, foundational root system of the Guildhall. Cinder stopped before a heavy wooden door, seamlessly carved into the side of a thick, living root that was as wide as a shuttlecraft. "This will be your team base for now," he said, his voice echoing slightly in the quiet hall. "The rooms down here are for the newer teams. They're not much, but they're dry, safe, and far enough away from the main hall that you won't be woken up by the night-shift teams coming and going."

He opened the door to reveal a single, large, circular chamber. The floor was covered in soft, sweet-smelling moss. Arranged around the edge were six round, woven-straw beds. In one corner, a sturdy wooden desk had been carved right out of the wall, and a small, curtained-off alcove revealed a few empty wooden chests for storage. A single, glowing crystal in the ceiling provided a soft, warm light. It was simple, rustic, and the most beautiful sight they had ever seen.

"Thank you, Cinder," Eva said, her voice thick with genuine gratitude.

The Charmander just nodded. "Get some rest, Team Starfall. Tomorrow, your new life begins."

He left, closing the door behind him and plunging the room into a cozy, quiet intimacy. For a long moment, the six of them just stood there, taking it all in. Zax walked to the door, testing its weight and the simple wooden lock, a ghost of a satisfied grunt escaping him. Anya, meanwhile, set their single survival satchel on the floor. With a quiet sigh, she reached inside and pulled out the dead shelter emitter, its casing cool and familiar. It had not worked since the morning before, but it was still a solid piece of Starfleet engineering, a ghost of their old life. She placed it carefully under the newly-carved desk, stowing their one piece of capital equipment out of the way before running a claw over the smooth surface of the desk itself, an engineer appreciating solid craftsmanship. Kenji fluttered down and immediately began fluffing one of the straw beds, chirping contentedly.

"We're safe," Eva said softly, and for the first time since the crash it sounded true.

"Our probability of long-term survival has increased by approximately 87.4 percent," T'Vella added, a hint of what might have been relief in her melodic voice.

Anya approached one of the beds. At first glance, it looked like a giant, oversized bird's nest, woven from thick, fragrant straw and reeds. "Primitive, but functional," she muttered to herself. It seemed crude and uncomfortable, but as she curled up in it, the woven material gave way with a soft rustle. The straw was filled with something soft and yielding—perhaps dried moss or cattail fluff—that shifted and molded to her hulking Typhlosion form like a high-tech memory foam mattress or a bean bag chair. After a few wiggles to get settled, she found it was surprisingly, deeply comfortable. "Remarkable," she murmured, impressed by the simple, effective engineering. Her flames dimmed to a gentle, hearth-like glow that filled the space with warmth.

Zax, ever the tactical officer, chose a bed near the door. He tested it warily, but found the same unexpected comfort. He settled in a low crouch, a position he could spring from in an instant, but the softness of the bed was already lulling his tense muscles into a state of relaxation he hadn't felt in days. Elara practically melted into her bed with a soft sigh, the simple comfort a powerful balm against the fear and uncertainty that had plagued her. For Kenji, the bed was perfect. "It's a nest!" he chirped happily, burrowing into the straw and fluff until only the top of his head was visible. T'Vella analyzed her own bed with a scientific curiosity. "Fascinating," she mused quietly. "A single, simple design that can ergonomically accommodate a wide variety of drastically different physiologies. The engineering is illogical, yet undeniably effective." One by one, the others discovered the same strange comfort in their own nest-like beds, the weariness of days finally claiming them as they found their own perfect sleeping positions.

Eva watched them, her family, safe and sheltered at last. They were no longer on the bridge of a starship, but in a room inside a tree. They were no longer Starfleet officers, but members of Team Starfall. It was a world away from everything they had ever known, but for the first time since the light had torn them from their reality, it felt like home. She lay down at last, and sleep took her before she could think to resist it.

"Zax, target that creature's energy source! Full phaser spread!" she commanded, her voice raw. Her human crew—her real crew—were at their stations, their faces illuminated by the frantic, pulsing red of the alert lights.

"Trying, Captain!" the Andorian tactical officer yelled back, his antennae twitching wildly as he fought a dying console. "The power conduits are unstable! I can't get a clean lock!"

"Anya, I need more power to the shields!"

"They're gone, Captain! The emitters are slag!" the engineer's voice screamed over the comm, the sound dissolving into a burst of static that was the sound of failing life support.

The ship lurched, a sickening, violent shudder that wasn't from an external impact. The inertial dampeners had failed. Eva felt a phantom sensation of weightlessness, then crushing gravity as her own human form was thrown against her restraints. Before she could react, the serpentine beast on the screen unleashed its attack. A beam of pure, golden energy slammed into the saucer section. The ship screamed, and consoles exploded. She watched in horror as the helm station detonated, the acrid smell of ozone filling her nostrils as her brilliant helmsman, Lieutenant Tanaka, was thrown from his seat like a rag doll.

"All hands, abandon ship! I repeat, abandon—" Eva's command was cut short as Zax finally got a lock.

"Firing phasers!" he yelled. Two beams of orange light lanced out, followed by the satisfying thump of a photon torpedo leaving the tube. The attacks struck the creature, scorching its scales and staggering it for a moment, but it was like throwing stones at a mountain. The beast roared, its anger now focused, absolute. It gathered another, larger sphere of energy.

"It's targeting engineering," Anya's voice crackled, filled with dread. "The warp core..."

The final beam struck. The bridge lights died, plunging them into a terrifying darkness lit only by the red alert strobes and the brilliant, blue-white glow emanating from the viewscreen—the light of their own annihilation. A piercing, world-ending sound screamed through the bridge—

Eva's eyes snapped open, her heart hammering against her ribs. She was ready to scream an order, to brace for impact, but the sound wasn't a klaxon. It was the gentle, melodic morning bell of the Guild. She was in her straw bed, safe. The rest of her crew, her new crew, were beginning to stir. It had not even happened like that. By the time the serpent came, the bridge had been empty, and she had been standing in a forest, watching. She quickly turned, burying her face in the soft, yielding straw, forcing her breathing to even out while the cold shame of it settled in her chest.

"Morning muster?" Kenji chirped, shaking the straw from his feathers.

As Eva pushed herself up, she caught Zax's gaze from across the room—a brief, questioning look. His Andorian intuition was sharp; she knew he had sensed something. The feeling of being seen, of her mask slipping for even a second, was all the motivation she needed to lock it firmly back into place.

"Unknown," she stated, her voice remarkably steady. She nodded towards the door, where T'Vella was already observing the hallway. "T'Vella, status?"

"There is a significant increase in foot traffic, Captain," the Gardevoir replied. "All personnel appear to be moving in a coordinated direction."

"When in doubt, follow the crowd," Eva decided. "Let's not be late for whatever this is."

They emerged from their quarters to find the halls of the great tree buzzing with energy. Dozens of exploration teams were streaming past, some chatting excitedly, others still shaking off sleep, but all heading purposefully downwards. Team Starfall fell into the living current, their presence still drawing curious glances, but no one stopped them. They were just another team heading to the morning briefing.

As they entered the main chamber of the Guild, they saw Cinder leaning against a support pillar, polishing the winged badge on his strap. He spotted them and gave a nod of approval.

"Figured it out on your own, huh?" he said with a smirk. "Good instincts. Some new teams spend their first week sleeping through the bell."

"Standard operating procedure," Zax grunted, though he felt a small measure of pride. "Observe and adapt."

Before they could continue, Anya, who had been carrying the tricorder, stopped dead. "Captain," she said, her voice filled with a quiet excitement. She held up the device. It had run almost flat the day before, spent on berries and scorch marks and one timed minute of Zax's light, and she had kept it switched off since to save what was left. Now the cracked screen showed a faint, flickering power bar with one more segment lit than last night, glowing a hopeful green.

"The battery is charging," she explained, her engineer's mind racing. T'Vella leaned in, her red eyes focused on the device.

"Fascinating," the Vulcan observed. "It must be the ambient energy from these light crystals. They are not only producing light; they are emitting a low-level, resonant field on a frequency the tricorder's induction charging can accept. It is inefficient. But it is enough."

It was a lifeline: a tangible link to their science, their methods, their very way of thinking, in a world that defied all logic. It was a foothold.

"Anya, T'Vella, that's brilliant," Eva said, her mind already calculating the possibilities. "Keep it exposed to the crystals whenever possible."

Their conversation was cut short as Guildmaster Ember-Smoke made his way to a raised platform at the front of the hall. A respectful silence fell over the assembled teams.

"Good morning, Pathfinders!" the old Torkoal's voice carried through the vast space. "Another beautiful day in Havenwood! The Kecleon market has a fresh stock of Reviver Seeds, so stock up before your journeys!"

He continued, outlining minor news and events before turning to the large mission board behind him. "Now, for today's tasks!" As he spoke, the team's professional instincts took over, and they analyzed the potential missions from their own unique perspectives.

"We have reports of an unusually aggressive swarm of Beedrill in the Silent Woods..." Zax's ears went forward at the mention of a swarm, his mind automatically calculating swarm dynamics and potential crowd-control tactics.

"...a request for escort services for a merchant heading to the coast..." Elara considered the logistics, the variables of protecting a non-combatant civilian in potentially hostile territory.

"...and a fascinating geological survey mission in the Crystal Caves." T'Vella's head came up, the mention of unique geological phenomena immediately capturing her scientific curiosity.

Eva watched it all, her mind sharp and analytical. She saw the flow of information, the allocation of resources, the clear chain of command. This was a system she understood. It was, in its own way, very much like Starfleet.

"And finally," the Guildmaster said, his old eyes seeming to find theirs in the crowd. "A task for our newest recruits. Team Starfall, please see me after the briefing."

A murmur went through the crowd. All eyes turned to them. Their life as an exploration team was about to begin.

As Guildmaster Ember-Smoke finished his main announcements and retreated from the platform, the Chatot from the reception desk fluttered up, a comically large stack of scrolls in his claws. "Alright, settle down, settle down!" he squawked, his voice sharp and piercing. He was Chirp, the Guild's long-suffering administrator, and this was his domain. His eye found Team Starfall in the crowd and narrowed: the six who had walked straight past his desk in the middle of the night. "But before we get to the daily postings, let's give a warm Havenwood welcome to our newest recruits, Team Starfall!"

A smattering of applause and a few cheerful "Hoorahs!" echoed through the hall, and one "*HOORAH!*" so loud it rattled the papers on the boards: Rumble, who by daylight saw no reason to hold back. Cinder's team gave them a nod of professional acknowledgement. It was a simple, welcoming gesture, a glimpse into the camaraderie and daily rhythm of Guild life.

"Alright, that's enough of that!" Chirp continued, his voice shifting into a rapid-fire, practiced cadence as he unfurled his list. "Time for the daily tasks! Let's see... a request from Spinda's Café for six Pecha berries, one lost pearl retrieval for a very distraught Shellder, a request to polish the town's light crystals..."

He continued on, his recitation of the minor errands and low-risk tasks a familiar, weary routine. Boredom settled over the main hall. The initial energy of the morning briefing dissipated, replaced by restless shuffling. Zax noticed a large Snorlax from another team, who had been leaning against a wall, was now sound asleep, a small bubble inflating and deflating from its nose with each thunderous snore.

"This part of the briefing appears to be... less than critical," T'Vella observed quietly, her logical mind struggling to find the efficiency in the process.

"It's for the rookies," Cinder whispered to them, having sidled over. "Gives the new teams a way to earn some coin and build a reputation without getting themselves flattened in a Mystery Dungeon. We all started with berry-picking."

Finally, after what felt like an eternity, the Chatot chirped, "And that's the lot! Dismissed!"

The hall erupted into motion as teams dispersed, some heading for the exit, others crowding the mission board to claim the more exciting jobs. The only one who didn't move was the Snorlax, who continued his nap, oblivious. His teammates, a pair of much smaller Mankey, sighed in unison and began trying to shove their colossal partner awake.

"Team Starfall," Cinder said, gesturing with his head. "The Guildmaster is waiting."

They navigated the bustling room and approached the platform where Ember-Smoke was conversing quietly with his Chatot assistant. The old Torkoal turned his slow, steady gaze upon them as they arrived.

"Ah, Team Starfall," he said. "I trust your first night in Havenwood was satisfactory?"

"It was, Guildmaster. We are grateful for your hospitality," Eva replied respectfully.

"Good. Hospitality is easy. Trust is earned," the Guildmaster stated, his tone shifting from pleasantry to business. "Every new team starts with a simple task. It allows us to gauge your abilities, your teamwork, and your character. It is a test."

He gestured with his head towards the exit. "Just east of Havenwood is a small forest known as the Murky Wood. Recently, the wild Pokémon there have become unusually timid and frightened. This fear... it is new. It began only a few days ago. They are hiding, and food is growing scarce for them. This is causing an imbalance."

A first official mission. Eva knew the feeling. The Guildmaster's assignment was logical, a test not of brute force, but of observation and discipline. She met his gaze, her expression becoming a neutral mask of professionalism. Her team was an unknown quantity, and this was his way of getting a baseline reading on their competence.

He continued, his voice steady. "Your mission is simple. Go to the Murky Wood. Discover the source of the Pokémon's fear. Do not engage it directly. Your task is reconnaissance only. Observe, analyze, and report back to me. Cinder's team has already cleared a path to the entrance. You will not face any significant threats on the way."

It was a classic Starfleet mission profile: observation, data collection, and non-interference unless absolutely necessary. It was a mission Eva understood completely.

"We understand, Guildmaster," Eva confirmed. "Reconnaissance only. We will not let you down."

"See that you don't," Ember-Smoke said. "Now go. Your first assignment awaits."

With a shared look of grim determination, Team Starfall turned. As they walked back through the now-bustling atrium, daylight falling softly from the skylights high in the trunk, they truly took in their surroundings for the first time. The map they had glimpsed in the dark was spread across the whole curve of the ceiling, charting vast coastlines and towering mountain ranges, and smaller ones were etched into the walls between the halls. Anya, the engineer, found her eyes drawn to a complex cutaway diagram of a dungeon, but a frustrated growl escaped her as she realized the detailed technical notes beside it were meaningless symbols.

"Captain," T'Vella stated, her voice a low, analytical hum. "An observation. Our auditory processing has adapted to the local language, but it appears our optical character recognition has not. We are, for all intents and purposes, illiterate."

The rest of the team paused, looking at the beautiful, incomprehensible script labeling the maps around them. The names of forests, caves, and mountain passes—all of it was alien.

"We can't read a signpost," Zax grunted, the tactical disadvantage of the situation hitting him with full force. "We're operating blind."

They stepped out of the Guildhall into the bright sun, carrying their first mission and the daunting fact of their own ignorance. Their new life had truly begun.
