Sleep did not bring peace.

Eva found herself adrift, a disembodied point of view floating near the ceiling of the Sagittarius's bridge. Below her was a scene of silent, frozen chaos. Her crew—her human crew—were slumped over their consoles, unconscious from the initial energy wave. Red alert lights painted the room in a silent, pulsing crimson. The only sound was the incessant, automated wail of alarms.

PROXIMITY ALERT. TERRAIN COLLISION IMMINENT. WARNING: WARP DRIVE OFFLINE. OVERSPEED CONDITION DETECTED. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FIELD: OFFLINE.

She turned her non-existent gaze to the main viewscreen. The ship had dropped out of warp nose-down above an alien world, and it was falling. Green and brown rushed up at them through a sheet of white-hot plasma that boiled across the edges of the screen. In the corner, the altimeter was a frantic blur of descending numbers, and the velocity readout beside it had gone red long ago, far past any speed a sane helmsman would bring a starship into atmosphere. Eva knew what an entry like this did to lesser craft. A pre-warp capsule would have lost its heat shield and come apart in seconds. A shuttlecraft would already be a streak of burning debris across the sky. The Sagittarius was still in one piece only because she was built in layers, and Eva watched those layers fail, one after another.

The deflector shields went first. They had snapped to full power the instant the golden light struck, and now the atmosphere hammered against them, a wall of compressed air turned to plasma. The shield grid flared from blue to white across the bow while the strength readout on the tactical display fell: sixty percent, forty, eighteen. The emitters along the saucer rim overloaded in a ripple of small, bright detonations, and the shields winked out.

The navigational deflector took over, doing the job it was built for on a scale no one had designed it for. At warp it swept dust and stray hydrogen from the ship's path; now it shoved an entire atmosphere aside. The dish burned an angry blue beneath the saucer, driving a cone of displaced air ahead of the hull and stripping away the worst of the heat. It bought them seconds, and its output gauge sank with every one of them.

What got past it hit the hull. On the forward sensor feed, the outer plating shifted from grey to a dull red, then to a glaring orange-white. Layered duranium-tritanium over a reinforced spaceframe, built to shrug off micrometeoroids and hard radiation. It had never been meant to take a planet's atmosphere at this speed. It held anyway, the way good Starfleet engineering held when everything else went wrong.

Beneath all of it, the one system that should have been holding the ship together was silent. On a healthy starship the structural integrity field never stopped running, a lattice of force threaded through every frame member and bulkhead, carrying loads that metal alone could never bear. Without it, a ship this size was only very strong metal falling very fast. The SIF generators had tripped when the golden wave tore through the warp field, and no one on the bridge was awake to bring them back. Eva heard the cost before she saw it: a deep, rising groan through the decks as the spaceframe took strain it was never meant to carry alone. Somewhere aft of the bridge, a support beam let go with a crack like a phaser shot.

In the last seconds, something on the ship still tried. The computer's terrain-avoidance routine, a last-ditch subroutine meant for shuttle bays and docking errors, found nobody at the helm and acted on its own. It fired both impulse engines at full thrust against the fall. They could not stop a ship of that mass, but they dragged the nose up, and the plunge became a long, flattening dive. Below, the forest opened into a valley floor of deep soil and giant ferns.

They hit at a shallow angle, a hammer blow followed by a long, grinding slide. The navigational deflector flared one last time, a brilliant bubble that caught the first of the kinetic force and spread it across the bow, buckling under the strain but keeping the hull from folding in on itself. The ground did the rest of the braking. The saucer plowed a furrow through soil and splintered trunks, shedding speed with every meter instead of all at once. Behind the deflector, with no integrity field to share the load, the frame took the shock on its own. Bulkheads sheared. Deck plating rippled like water. A split second later, the indicator for the inertial dampeners blinked from green to red and went dark.

It was a predictable outcome. The laws of physics were absolute. Below her, the unconscious bodies of her crew were flung from their seats like dolls. Kenji was thrown against his console with bone-shattering force. Zax was hurled across the bridge, his body striking the tactical station with a sickening thud. Elara, T'Vella, even her own human form—all were subjected to the raw, unforgiving violence of the crash.

The slide ended with the bow buried in the far side of the furrow and the saucer tilted at a sickening angle. The groaning slowed, then stopped. On the bridge displays, damage reports scrolled in a red flood: fractured frame members on six decks, ruptured conduits, hull breaches in the secondary hull. But the hull itself had held its shape. Every layer had taken its share before it failed: the shields had borne the worst of the fire, the deflector had pushed the air aside and cushioned the strike, the plating had kept its form, and the long slide had spent the rest. The inside of the ship was wrecked. The outside was still, unmistakably, a starship. One impulse engine was still firing weakly against nothing, and the emergency lights flickered back on along the saucer.

Then, in that broken stillness, a new light appeared. It was not the harsh red of the alarms, but a soft, pure gold. It started as a single point of light in the center of the bridge, then expanded, a gentle, searching luminescence.

The golden light was intelligent. It moved with purpose, darting from one unconscious crew member to the next. It would hover over a body for a moment, a silent, intense scan, before moving on. It swept over the entire bridge crew, then seemed to expand its search, the light flowing through the very walls of the ship as if they were glass. Eva felt it pass through her own disembodied form, a feeling of being analyzed, weighed, and measured by an intelligence beyond comprehension. The light was sporadic, chaotic, as if it was struggling to lock onto a specific target through the interference of the ship's own damaged systems.

Finally, after what felt like an eternity, the chaotic searching stopped. The light seemed to make a decision. It split into six distinct points of brighter, more focused light. One settled on her own unconscious human form. The others found T'Vella, Zax, Anya in engineering, Elara, and Kenji.

The six points of light intensified, blossoming into brilliant, golden orbs that completely enveloped the chosen bodies. The light was brighter than a starship's transporter beam, more pure, more absolute. Then, with a silent, inward rush, the orbs collapsed into themselves, imploding into nothingness. Where six Starfleet officers had been, there was now only empty space. They had been taken, excised from reality with surgical precision.

The dream ended.

Eva's eyes snapped open. She was an Absol, lying on a bed of straw, her heart pounding. The dream had not been a memory of the battle with Rayquaza. It had been something else. A different truth. They hadn't been beamed out by a fellow officer in a last, heroic act. They had been... selected. Chosen and removed by the same impossible force that had brought them here. They had survived the crash, only to be taken before the ship's final destruction. The rest of the crew... they had been left behind, still unconscious, to face the wrath of the sky god and the agony of the warp core breach alone. The thought was a new, more terrible kind of horror.

She lay there, paralyzed by the revelation, as the first hints of dawn began to filter through the cracks in the door. Just as she was forcing herself to sit up, to bury the truth under layers of command discipline, the clear, bright chime of the Guild's morning bell echoed through their room.

The sound made her jump, but it also served as an anchor, pulling her back to the present. Around the room, the rest of her team began to stir, groaning and stretching, the bell their unwelcome alarm clock after a long day and a late night.

"Morning, Captain," Elara said softly, her voice gentle as she sat up. She paused, tilting her head, her empathic senses immediately picking up on the turbulent storm Eva was trying to suppress. "Are you alright?"

Eva turned to her Coordinator, schooling her features into a neutral mask. "I'm fine, Elara. Just a long night."

But the deception didn't work. Not anymore. Elara took a hesitant step closer, her large, expressive eyes filled with concern. "It's your eyes, Captain," she whispered, so the others wouldn't hear. "Your human face... it was very good at hiding what you felt. But as an Absol... your eyes are so large, so... clear. I can see the storm in them. I can feel it."

Eva was taken aback. She had spent her entire career cultivating a commander's poker face, a mask of calm authority that never cracked under pressure. To be read so easily, to have her inner turmoil so plainly visible, was a new and deeply unsettling vulnerability. She had forgotten that her new form came with new tells.

"I had a difficult night," Eva admitted, her voice low. "The memories of the crash... they were vivid." It was a half-truth, but it was the only one she could offer without revealing the new, horrifying layer of their predicament.

Elara didn't press, but the concern in her eyes remained. She simply nodded, a silent offer of support that Eva was grateful for.

They gathered their gear and headed for the morning briefing. The atmosphere in the main hall was more subdued than usual. The typical boisterous chatter was replaced by low, worried murmurs. As Chirp the Chatot flew to the platform, a heavy silence fell over the room.

"Attention, Pathfinders," Chirp began, his usual cheerful squawk replaced by a somber, serious tone. "We have a critical update. A Silver-Tier team returned late last night from a patrol of the Murky Wood." He took a deep breath. "The Murky Wood is a total loss. The Shadow Corruption has spread beyond containment. The entire forest is now a 'Dead Zone,' emanating a powerful, corrupting aura. Any Pokémon that remains within its borders for too long will succumb."

A wave of shock and fear rippled through the assembled teams. Elara flinched, feeling the collective spike of anxiety.

"Therefore," Chirp continued, his voice grim, "a new directive has been issued by the Guild. Any corrupted Pokémon found operating outside the Dead Zone, particularly those approaching Havenwood, are to be considered an active, contagious threat. Elite teams are now authorized to... neutralize these threats on sight. This is a culling, Pathfinders. A necessary measure to slow the spread and protect this town."

The word "culling" hung in the air, cold and ugly. This was no longer about rescue or exploration. This was about sanitation. This was about putting down former friends and neighbors to stop a plague. Zax's hand instinctively went to his holstered phaser, his expression hard as stone. This was a reality of war he understood all too well.

"This task is restricted to Gold-Tier teams and above," Chirp clarified, much to the visible relief of the younger teams. "All other teams are to avoid the Murky Wood and its immediate surroundings at all costs."

He then looked directly at Team Starfall, his gaze sharp. "Team Starfall, you have a new priority assignment from the Guildmaster." The direct address immediately silenced the whispers, all eyes turning to them once more. "Your engineering projects are now of the highest importance. The artisans in town have been instructed to prioritize your commissions above all others. Your task is to complete the construction and calibration of your new devices with all possible speed. Report to Fia in the archive for any necessary resources or information. That is all. Dismissed."

The briefing ended, leaving a heavy, fearful silence in its wake. While other teams dispersed with worried looks, Team Starfall stood united, their own mission now cast in a new, more urgent light. Their private research project had just become the Guild's best hope for a new weapon in a war they were desperately losing.

As the other teams began to move, Eva and her crew remained still for a moment, a silent, cohesive unit in the center of the hall. Nobody spoke. The new directive was a heavy burden, but at least it was a clear one.

"So," Anya said under her breath. "We've been promoted from explorers to the R&D department."

"A logical progression," T'Vella noted. "Our unique technical knowledge makes us the most qualified for the task."

Before they could discuss it further, a familiar voice cut in. "Top priority, huh? You guys just keep getting more interesting."

They turned to see Team Cinder approaching, their expressions no longer suspicious, but openly curious. The other teams gave them a wide berth, a mixture of respect and apprehension in their eyes.

"So the Murky Wood is a total loss," Cinder said, shaking his head. "We were afraid of that. The corruption was spreading like wildfire." He looked at Eva. "And now you're the Guild's top secret project. What kind of 'devices' are you working on that are more important than patrols?"

"That's classified," Eva replied, her voice leaving no room for argument. But she saw the genuine curiosity in Cinder's eyes, not a challenge, but the concern of a fellow soldier. "Let's just say we're working on a way to better understand the enemy. A new type of sensor."

Leaf, the Grovyle, nodded slowly. "A new way to track the corruption... that would be a game-changer. We're fighting blind out there."

"Exactly," Eva confirmed.

The exchange was brief, but it settled something. They were no longer just another team. They were a special project, a wild card, the Guild's secret weapon, and every team in the hall knew it. The pressure was immense. Still, as they turned to leave, heading not for the exit but for the archive and Fia, they walked with a renewed sense of purpose. The war had a new front, and they were the engineers who would build its weapons.

The noise of the main hall faded behind them, and in the quieter passages beyond it, the morning's news seemed to grow louder.

"A culling," Zax growled as they walked. "It has come to that already."

"It is a logical, if regrettable, escalation," T'Vella said. "The corruption is behaving like a contagion. Quarantine and sterilization of infected elements is a standard protocol for containing a plague."

"These aren't just 'infected elements,' T'Vella," Anya countered, her voice heavy. "They're Pokémon. They're people."

"Which is why our project is now the Guild's top priority," Eva said, her voice cutting through the somber discussion and refocusing them. "Chirp's orders were clear: report to Fia in the archive for resources. Our first priority is to acquire the tools for the Control Glove. Let's go."

The plan was set. They bypassed the mess hall and made their way down the quiet, little-used corridor that led to the archive, the heavy ironwood door waiting for them at the end. As before, it swung open silently at their approach, as if Fia had been expecting them.

The Delphox Archivist was standing by her massive desk, a single, ancient scroll open before her. She looked up as they entered, her wise eyes taking in the full, determined team.

"Team Starfall," she greeted, her voice a soft whisper. "I had a feeling you would be paying me a visit. Your minds are... very loud this morning. Full of questions and new discoveries."

"Fia," Eva began with a respectful nod. "We have two objectives. First, a practical matter. Our research indicates we require a mortar and pestle, one capable of grinding crystalline material, to proceed with our work."

Fia's lips curved into a faint, knowing smile. "Ah, so you have deciphered the chapter on essence transference. Very impressive." She gestured with her wand towards a dusty corner of the archive stacked with old chests and forgotten equipment. "The Guild has many such tools from older eras of exploration. I believe you will find what you need in the third chest from the left. Consider it on loan from the archives."

Anya and Zax moved to the chest, their practical task underway. Eva, T'Vella, and Elara approached the desk.

"Our second objective is more... complex," T'Vella said, carefully placing the enchantment tome on the desk. She opened it to the annotated table of contents. "We have discovered a secondary author within this text. Their contributions," she continued, her voice precise, "are not written with a brush, but with a tool of advanced precision. The organizational structure of the index, the chemical composition of the ink... they are indicative of a scientific methodology identical to our own. We believe another individual from a technologically advanced society has been here before us. We need to know who they were."

Fia looked at the page, her expression unreadable. She showed no surprise, only a deep, thoughtful recognition. "So, the ghost has finally revealed itself to you," she said softly. She ran a slender finger over the sharp, angular script of the annotations. "These markings are rare. Very rare. They are the hallmark of an organization that has not been active in this region for a very, very long time."

She looked up, her gaze meeting Eva's. "The stories are fragmented, dismissed by most as legend. But the archives remember. Long ago, before the Pathfinder Guild unified the guilds, there was another group. They were explorers and scientists, much like you, but their understanding of the world was... different. Deeper. They could fuse the energy of this world with a science that was not of this world. They called themselves the Paradigm Research Group."

The name echoed in the dusty silence of the archive. Paradigm. A perfect model, an example.

"They were led by a being of incredible power and intellect," Fia continued, her voice taking on the quality of a storyteller reciting an ancient epic. "A being who, like you, was not born of this world. The records are incomplete, but they suggest this being arrived during a time of great upheaval, a past calamity that has since faded into myth. The Paradigm Group created incredible technologies—artifacts of immense power, some of which, like your badges, still exist, their true origins forgotten. But the group eventually splintered, its members scattering, its knowledge lost to time. All that remains are whispers, legends, and a few rare artifacts bearing their strange, angular script."

She looked at the book, then back at Eva. "You are not just following in a ghost's footsteps, Captain. You have stumbled upon the legacy of a legend. And in doing so, you have placed yourselves at the very center of a mystery far older than the Shadow Corruption you have been tasked to fight."

With her story told, Fia reached out and, with a practiced, delicate touch, began to flip through the pages of the enchantment book. She didn't stop at the chapters on orb modification or essence transference. She went all the way to the end, past the final appendix written in the native, flowing script.

The last section of the book was jarringly different. The pages here were a brilliant, sterile white, made of a smooth, polymer-like material instead of the rough parchment of the rest of the tome. They were pristine, untouched by age or moisture. And upon them, there was no handwriting.

Instead, the pages were covered in dense, microscopic text, a jumbled mess of numbers and symbols arranged in precise, unending columns. It was not the Pokémon script, but Federation Standard. The text was impossibly sharp and clean, clearly etched onto the pages by a high-precision laser. This went on for the last ninety or so pages of the book.

Anya, having returned from the storage chests with a heavy stone mortar and pestle, leaned in, her eyes wide. "That's... that's a data dump," she breathed. "It's a hard copy backup. It looks like... hexadecimal code. Or something more complex. This isn't a chapter; it's a computer file printed onto paper."

"The ghost's final gift," T'Vella surmised, her mind reeling with the implications. "They must have lacked a proper storage device, so they used their phaser on its lowest setting to etch their data onto these blank pages. An act of desperation... and genius."

Fia looked at them, her expression one of deep curiosity. "For as long as this book has been in Havenwood’s possession, this final section has been a complete mystery. None of our scholars could make any sense of it. They believed it to be a curse, or a meaningless scrawl left by a madman. We never imagined it was... a language."

As the team stared at the wall of numbers, T'Vella's keen eyes noticed something. In the bottom corner of the first polymer page, there was a small, meticulously etched symbol. It was a downward-pointing diamond shape with a small circle inside it, topped by an open chevron. Beneath the symbol, almost too small to read, were three letters in Federation Standard: PRG.

"Fia," T'Vella said, her voice low and steady. "The Paradigm Research Group... did they have a symbol? A logo?"

Fia's eyes widened slightly. "The records are sparse, but yes. The few authenticated artifacts we have bear a maker's mark. It was said to represent a guiding star, a beacon in the darkness."

T'Vella pointed her slender finger at the tiny symbol on the corner of the data page. "Does it look like this?"

Fia leaned in, her gaze sharp. A slow, astonished smile spread across her face. "By the great Dialga," she whispered. "It's a perfect match. The Beacon of Paradigm." She looked up at the team, her mind connecting the dots. "The ghost who annotated this book... they were not just a member of the Paradigm Research Group. They were clearly a high-ranking one. The original text is ancient, but these annotations, the table of contents, the data dump... this is their addition. Their attempt to refine and preserve this knowledge."

The team stared at the tiny symbol, this "Beacon." It was more than just a clue; it was a signature. A brand. It confirmed that the advanced technology they had encountered, from their badges to the very structure of the book they held, all stemmed from this one, mysterious source. The ghost wasn't just a fellow traveler; they were the founder of a scientific revolution in this world. And this book, with its encrypted final chapter, was their magnum opus.

Anya's eyes suddenly lit up, the flames on her neck flaring with a jolt of pure engineering insight. "Wait a minute," she said, her voice bright with excitement. "A data dump... machine code... T'Vella, we're fools!"

"Explain, Chief," Eva said, turning her attention to the Typhlosion.

"We've been trying to translate this world's magic into our science," Anya said, gesturing to the book. "But the ghost already did it! They translated their own knowledge, their own data, into a format that could be physically stored here. We don't need to learn a new language to read this part. We just need a computer." She unslung the satchel from her shoulder and carefully pulled out the tricorder. "We have a computer. A machine that excels at decoding anything and everything."

The realization hit the rest of the team like a phaser on stun. They had been so focused on the handwritten annotations and the native script that none of them had ever paged through to the very end. The final ninety pages weren't a linguistic puzzle; they were a technical one.

"Anya, you're a genius," Eva breathed.

T'Vella carefully held the book open to the first page of the data dump. Anya activated the tricorder, its familiar hum filling the dusty archive. She aimed the scanner at the wall of laser-etched hexadecimal code. The tricorder's screen, once displaying simple power percentages, now flickered to life with cascading lines of decryption algorithms.

"It's working," Anya whispered. "The tricorder recognizes the base code. It's a standard Federation data encryption, but with a custom cipher. It's complex... very complex. It will take time to break. But it's possible."

They stood there, a bridge crew from a fallen starship huddled around a book of magic in an ancient archive, using 25th-century technology to read what the ghost had left behind. For the first time, they had a direct line to their predecessor.

Anya was already running estimates for a brute-force attack when the tricorder gave a soft series of beeps. It was a notification chime, one she hadn't heard since before the crash.

The screen, though cracked, displayed a message in clear, Federation Standard text: [AUDIO FILE DECODED. READY FOR PLAYBACK.]

"The data dump," Eva breathed, her heart beginning to pound. "It broke the encryption already?"

"Negative, Captain," T'Vella corrected, peering at the screen over Anya's shoulder. "The tricorder's log indicates it did not need to run decryption algorithms. The data on the first page was not encrypted; it was raw, uncompressed audio data, simply etched into the polymer. The tricorder has converted the physical etching back into a playable audio file."

The team, along with Fia, gathered around, a tense silence falling over the room. This was it. The first direct message from their mysterious predecessor. With a deep breath, Anya tapped the 'play' icon on the tricorder's screen.

A voice, clear and uncorrupted by static, filled the small room. It was a young man's voice, tinged with a weariness that belied its youthful tone, yet it carried an undercurrent of unwavering resolve.

"Greetings, listener. If you are hearing this, that means this world either finally reached the information age, or that once again, the world is in danger. If it's the latter, or former really... Then continue. You are either curious enough to give this a shot, or you got lucky like I did, what with being fortunate enough to hold onto some tech on my arrival."

At that line, Anya's head snapped up. Her gaze darted to the damaged tricorder in her paws, then to the phaser on Zax's hip. They had salvaged their tech from a wreck, a desperate act of recovery. The ghost’s words implied something different—that he had managed to preserve his technology through the transition. The implication was enormous. If he knew how, the knowledge would be more valuable than any artifact they had yet discovered.

The team exchanged stunned glances, a new, intense question now burning alongside all the others. The voice, the cadence, the terminology—it was unmistakably human. Fia's ears twitched, her expression one of intense, focused fascination.

"I am Alan Hexfury, species, Riolu. Current position at the time of recording, CEO of the Paradigm Research Group, Exo-Reality Gate Command, and commander of the modified Raider II Class Corvette- my baby The ATLAS. Now depending on when this is found, some of, or all of these might not exist anymore. I can only leave names for now, but if the world is truly in danger, then work towards unlocking the next page and most certainly read the entire book if you can’t. It's long and hard, but it has a lot of insight to share, I even left little notes to help you out. You might not even need what I have to offer, this book is a treasure trove and one hell of a jumping off point. Shape the world around you, is always the easiest answer here. Create, it’s what we do."

The audio file ended, leaving an echoing silence in its wake. They stared at the tricorder as if it were a holy relic. They had a name. Alan Hexfury. And a species—Riolu. The species that evolved into Zax's own. Hexfury had survived, and then gone on to found organizations, command vessels, and master the technology of this world well enough to leave this message behind.

"Paradigm Research Group," T'Vella whispered, the name Fia had spoken now given a face, a voice.

"Exo-Reality Gate Command," Zax added, his mind racing through the tactical implications of such a title. "He wasn't just exploring. He was dealing with inter-dimensional travel."

Anya, however, shook her head, her engineer's mind already racing. "Wait," she rumbled, her brow furrowed. She held the tricorder, her thumb claw quickly navigating through its diagnostic menus. The others watched as she pulled up the Starfleet Engineering database, a library of technical specifications downloaded to every ship's core computer. "A Raider II Class Corvette? The ATLAS?" Her claw tapped on the screen, scrolling rapidly through a list of ship classes. Galaxy... Intrepid... Sovereign... Odyssey... The names of the Federation's most famous vessels flashed by. "Those aren't Starfleet designations. I've cross-referenced every ship class in the Federation registry from the 22nd century to the 25th. Nothing matches."

T'Vella's eyes widened slightly as she processed the new data. "Anya is correct. The technology is familiar—phaser-etched data, the use of 'species' as a classification—but the terminology is foreign. It is a logical conclusion that Alan, while human, was not a member of Starfleet."

The implication took a moment to land. "Then this world..." Eva began, her voice barely a whisper. "The force that brought us here... it's not limited to our reality. It's pulling from others, too."

The universe, which had already felt enormous, suddenly became infinitely more complex. They were not just lost; they were one data point in a much larger, more terrifying equation. The ghost wasn't a Starfleet officer at all. He was a castaway from another, parallel universe, one with technology similar enough to be recognizable, but different enough to be alien.

Eva stood, her expression a mixture of awe and grim determination. The ghost was real. And he had left them a mission. "He said to unlock the next page," she said, her voice quiet but firm. "That's our new directive. T'Vella, Anya, how long will it take to break the real encryption?"

"It's a complex, custom cipher," Anya replied, her eyes already back on the tricorder's screen. "But now that we have a baseline, and with the tricorder's processing power... it's possible. It will take time. Days, maybe weeks of continuous processing."

"Then we have no time to waste," Eva declared. Their personal projects, the Control Glove, the tricorder housing—they were no longer just about survival. They were about following the path laid out by Alan Hexfury, about uncovering the legacy of the Paradigm Research Group, and about preparing for the danger he had warned them of. The ghost had spoken, and Team Starfall had answered the call.

Eva turned to the Delphox archivist, who had listened to the recording with an unreadable expression. "Fia, thank you for your help. We have what we need for now."

"The path you walk is an old one, and a dangerous one," Fia said, her voice a soft, cautionary whisper. "The Paradigm Research Group delved into secrets that this world has long since tried to forget. Be careful what you seek."

"We will," Eva replied with a firm nod.

Zax hefted the stone mortar and pestle. As Anya turned to leave, her engineer's eye caught something else half-buried under a pile of old canvases: a heavy, flat-topped desk made of dark, petrified wood. It was scarred and ancient, but its surface was perfectly level, and its legs were thick and sturdy.

"Wait a minute," she said, walking over and brushing the dust off its surface. "Captain, look at this. It's a proper workbench. Stable, solid... perfect for the delicate work of grinding the orbs and assembling the gloves. Far better than the floor of our room."

"An old enchanting desk, from before the Guild standardized its equipment," Fia supplied. "It has not been used in centuries."

"It will be now," Anya declared. "Zax, give me a hand."

Without a word, the two powerhouses of the team hoisted the heavy desk, its ancient wood groaning in protest. It was an awkward, cumbersome load, but they managed it.

"Thank you again, Fia," Eva said, giving the archivist a final, respectful nod.

Fia simply inclined her head, a flicker of amusement in her eyes. May your path be lit, Team Starfall.

As they turned to leave, the heavy ironwood door swung open before them of its own accord. The team paused and glanced back at the serene Delphox.

"Psychics are cool," Kenji chirped, awestruck.

Fia's faint smile was their only reply.

Laden with the ancient book, their borrowed tools and a heavy, forgotten piece of history that would become their new engineering station, the team began the awkward trek out of the archive. The trip down the spiraling ramp into the roots was a comical struggle. Zax and Anya, for all their strength, had to coordinate every step to maneuver the wide desk around the curved walls.

"A little to the left, Zax!" Anya grunted, her flames flaring with exertion. "My left, not your left!"

"My left is your left. We are facing the same direction!" Zax shot back, his muscles straining.

The others trailed behind, their minds buzzing. For a while, the Shadow Corruption and everything they had just been asked to carry seemed to fade, replaced by the pure excitement of a new frontier. They had a name, a history, and a set of instructions from a ghost who spoke their language.

By the time they reached their quarters, the descent had drained the last of their energy, but the excitement held. The desk went through the doorway with a claw's width to spare on either side.

"Alright," Anya grunted as she and Zax eased it into the room. "Let's set up our new engineering section."

They placed the salvaged desk against the curved wall, beside the built-in one. Together the two surfaces formed a large, L-shaped workbench that followed the room's natural arc and turned a corner of the chamber into a dedicated research and development area.

"The built-in desk will serve as the primary research station," Scholar T'Vella stated, already placing the heavy enchantment tome upon its surface. "I will continue my translation and theoretical analysis here."

"And this," Anya said, running a clawed hand over the smooth, cool surface of the petrified wood desk with a proprietary air, "is the fabrication station. All practical experiments, material analysis, and assembly will happen here." For the first time since the crash, Anya felt a flicker of her old self. She wasn't just a Typhlosion; she was a Chief Engineer, and this was her workshop.

With the workspace established, there was no time to waste. The rest of the team gathered around as Anya carefully unwrapped one of the newly purchased Slumber Orbs. It shimmered with a soft, soporific light, the mist within swirling lazily. She placed the heavy stone mortar on the desk, its weight reassuringly solid.

"The book was clear," T'Vella advised, looking over her shoulder. "The grinding process must be slow and deliberate. A sudden application of force could cause the orb's imbued essence to discharge unpredictably."

Anya nodded, her usual boisterous energy replaced by a focused, professional calm. She placed the Slumber Orb into the mortar. Taking the heavy pestle in her powerful claws, she began to work. She didn't slam or pound, but applied a slow, steady, crushing pressure.

The first sound was a sharp crack, like breaking glass, as the orb's outer shell gave way. A collective gasp went through the team as the shimmering light of the orb intensified for a split second. Then, with a sound like a sudden decompression, a soft fizz, the mist inside the orb escaped from the crack, dissipating into the air with the faint scent of lavender. The orb's gentle glow died instantly, leaving behind a dull, inert-looking piece of glassy rock.

"Scrap!" Anya growled, recoiling. "The essence discharged! I've ruined it!"

"Did we lose the power?" Kenji chirped, looking worried.

"Hold," T'Vella said, her voice calm. She quickly flipped through the pages of the enchantment book, her eyes scanning the text and diagrams until she found the relevant section. "Here. The ghost's annotation on material preparation." She leaned closer, her finger tracing the fine script.

"The text is complex," she began, sounding out the words with careful deliberation. "'The... imbued essence... of an orb... must be... discharged...'" She paused, looking at the accompanying diagram, which showed a glowing orb cracking and then becoming dull.

Anya, looking over her shoulder, immediately grasped the visual language. "Look, the energy leaves," she said, pointing a claw at the schematic. "He's showing it's a necessary step. The orb has to be 'emptied' first."

"A logical interpretation," T'Vella agreed, the visual context helping her parse the next part of the sentence. "The purpose is...'preparing its unique crystalline matrix... to serve as a blank slate...'"

Anya's eyes lit up, the engineering principle clicking into place. "A blank slate! A conduit! So the goal isn't to preserve the original enchantment at all. It's to prepare the raw material." She looked from the dead orb on their desk to the book, a triumphant grin spreading across her face. "It's not a failed component; it's a prepared one."

The tension went out of the room. "So... I was supposed to do that," Anya said sheepishly.

"It would appear our definition of 'failure' in this context was incorrect," T'Vella stated. "The discharge is part of the engineering process. Please proceed, Chief."

With a renewed sense of confidence, Anya returned to her task. The rest of the orb, now just a dull, glassy material, began to crumble under the immense, controlled pressure. A fine, shimmering powder, the color of twilight, began to fill the bottom of the mortar. The process was painstaking. Anya worked with the precision of a surgeon, her powerful muscles making minute adjustments to ensure the powder was ground to a perfectly fine, consistent grain. T'Vella observed, occasionally referencing the diagrams in the book, offering quiet guidance. Zax stood guard, his aura sense alert for any unexpected energy fluctuations. Elara and Kenji watched, mesmerized by the strange, alchemical process.

Slowly, the shimmering orb was reduced to a small, glowing pile of fine, purple dust. It was their first successfully processed component, the first raw material they had created themselves. It was a small, yet monumental step. They were no longer just using the tools of this world; they were beginning to deconstruct and understand them, paving the way for the creation of their own.

"Component one is prepared," Anya announced, a deep satisfaction in her voice. She carefully set the mortar aside. From the team satchel, she retrieved a small, flat piece of fire-resistant ceramic they had commissioned from Breaker the blacksmith. Etched into its surface were six shallow, perfectly circular indentations—the molds for the Orb Dust lenses.

She placed the ceramic mold on the desk next to the mortar. "Now for the delicate part," she said, her voice dropping to a low, focused murmur. "The book says the dust must be heated until it becomes a molten, glass-like slurry. Then it can be poured into the molds to form the lenses."

"A controlled heat source is required," T'Vella noted, referencing the text. "Too much heat will vaporize the material. Too little, and it will not fuse correctly."

Anya nodded. "I can do it."

She leaned over the desk, her expression one of intense concentration. She took a deep breath, focusing her internal energy not into a chaotic blast, but into a tiny, controlled point. She opened her mouth slightly, and a single, small ember, no larger than her claw, dropped from her lips into the mortar. It didn't explode or flare; it simply glowed with a steady, intense heat. She then exhaled a slow, continuous stream of superheated air over the ember, using her breath to precisely control its temperature, much like a blacksmith using a bellows. The flames on her neck flickered in time with her breathing, a visual indicator of her focus.

The rest of the team watched in silence, holding their breath. This was a level of control Anya had never before displayed. The dust began to glow, its color shifting from twilight purple to a bright, molten silver. The scent of ozone filled the air as the ancient material began to melt, its latent, magical properties being reshaped by the will of a Starfleet engineer. The first true step in creating their own hybrid technology had begun.

The process was agonizingly slow. Anya had to maintain a perfect, steady temperature, her entire focus poured into the single ember in the mortar. The minutes stretched on, the only sounds in the room the soft crackle of the ember and Anya's deep, rhythmic breathing.

Just as the last of the powder began to liquefy into a shimmering, molten slurry, a sharp knock echoed from their room's heavy wooden door.

The team jumped, startled out of their focused silence. Zax was on his feet in an instant, moving to a defensive position beside the door. "Identify yourself," he called.

"Stores," a brisk voice answered. "Delivery for Team Starfall. Open up, I've got four more rooms after yours."

Eva gave Zax a nod. He unlatched the door and opened it, and found himself looking up. A Kangaskhan filled most of the doorway, a large cloth-wrapped bundle tucked under one arm and a flat slate hanging from a cord around her neck. From the pouch at her belly, a small, round face peered past Zax into the room with enormous interest.

"Tally. Quartermaster," the Kangaskhan said, as if it were one word. "Anything that comes into this Guild for a team comes through my Stores, and anything that goes out leaves the same way. Breaker's apprentice dropped this off ten minutes ago. Guildmaster's orders say yours go to the front of the line and down to the roots the moment they arrive, day or night." Her tone suggested she had opinions about that, and was keeping them to herself for now. She held out the bundle. "Breaker says the metalwork was a real challenge, and he enjoyed every minute. His words."

Zax took the bundle. It was heavier than it looked.

Tally lifted the slate and a stub of chalk. "Mark for it."

Eva glanced down at her own paws, then at T'Vella. The Gardevoir stepped forward and wrote her name in neat Federation Standard. Tally turned the slate toward the crystal light and studied the letters for a long moment.

"That's a new one," she said finally, and copied the shape into her own column of tallies without further comment. "If this room runs out of floor," she added, with a glance at the petrified desk taking up half of it, "the Stores keep anything for a registered team. No charge at Bronze."

The little one in her pouch had found Kenji. It stretched out a stubby arm toward his tail feathers, and Kenji hopped back onto Anya's shoulder with a startled chirp.

"Pip. Hands," Tally said, without looking down, and the arm withdrew. With a short nod to Eva, she was gone, her heavy footsteps already moving on to the next door.

Zax latched the door behind her and stood listening until the footsteps faded. "She knew which room was ours," he said. "She knew what was coming, who sent it, and when it arrived. And she keeps a written record."

"She's a quartermaster, Zax." Anya had already turned back to the mortar, one eye on the slurry's glow. "This place has a supply chain. Deliveries, storage, sign-offs." Her flames gave a pleased flicker. "Do you know how long I've wanted a requisitions officer who brings parts down to engineering instead of making me fill out a form in triplicate?"

"You still had to sign," Kenji pointed out from her shoulder, smoothing his tail feathers. "And that kid nearly took my tail."

"I signed," T'Vella said. "In Federation Standard." She paused. "Which is now part of the Guild's permanent inventory records."

Eva considered that, then let it go. "So is our registration. Half of Havenwood already thinks we're strange. Every starbase we ever served on ran on its logistics, and this place is no different. Better to have Tally on our side of the ledger."

They carried the bundle to the research desk and unwrapped it carefully.

Inside were two items. The first was the new tricorder housing. It was a masterpiece of function-over-form design, a heavy, dark alloy case with a perfectly fitted hinge and a satisfyingly solid locking clasp. The compartment for the power crystal was exactly to Anya's specifications. The second item was a single, dark leather glove.

"Just one?" Kenji chirped, looking at the bundle.

"It's my glove," Zax said, recognizing the four-fingered design. He picked it up. It was tough but flexible, and on the palm were the three recessed, circular molds, perfectly placed.

Anya examined the craftsmanship. "It seems the Leavanny had an existing pattern for a Lucario's paw that she could modify. T'Vella's three-fingered Gardevoir hand is a more unique anatomy; hers will have to be crafted from scratch. This sped up the timeline for Zax's."

They now had all the components. The molten Orb Dust slurry, shimmering in the mortar. The ceramic mold, ready to form the lenses. And the first of their custom-built interfaces. The time for theory and preparation was over. The time for fabrication had arrived.

"Alright," Anya said, her focus absolute. "T'Vella, assist me. We need to pour before it cools."

With the utmost care, Anya lifted the heavy stone mortar. T'Vella steadied the ceramic plate and called out each fill line. Anya tipped the mortar slowly, and a perfect, shimmering stream of liquid Orb Dust flowed into the first of the circular molds on the ceramic plate. She repeated the process twice more, filling three of the six molds.

"That's all the dust from the first orb," Anya reported, setting the now-empty mortar down. "We'll need to grind another for the second glove."

They watched the three small, molten pools. They expected them to cool and harden into flat discs. Instead, something strange happened. As the liquid cooled, its surface tension seemed to fight against gravity in an unnatural way. The top of the liquid pulled inwards, creating a perfectly smooth, shallow, upward-facing curve.

"Fascinating," T'Vella whispered, her scientific curiosity overriding her Vulcan composure. "The material is self-shaping. It is forming a concave lens of its own accord."

Anya leaned in, her eyes wide with an engineer's delight. "It's a perfect basic focusing lens. The shape, the curvature... it's built to gather energy and concentrate it toward a single point. The material wants to do this."

The ghost's annotations had told them how to build the interface, but not this. Even Alan Hexfury hadn't thought to mention that the material took part in its own making.

Then there was nothing left to do but wait, the hardest duty there is for a crew of action-oriented officers.

"The book indicates the lenses must cool naturally," T'Vella said, her eyes fixed on the three small, shimmering pools of silvery liquid. "Any attempt to accelerate the process could create micro-fractures in the crystalline matrix, rendering them useless."

"So we wait," Eva said, though the inactivity clearly chafed at her. "In the meantime, we can prepare for the next step."

Seeing an opportunity to be useful, Elara retrieved the old, damaged tricorder and the new housing they had just received. "While we wait for the lenses to cool," she said softly, "I can begin transferring the tricorder's components into the new housing. It will save time."

Eva nodded. "Good initiative, Coordinator."

Elara sat at the built-in desk, carefully placing the tricorder and its new shell on a soft cloth. Using a small, pointed rock as a makeshift tool, she began to painstakingly work at the seams of the damaged casing, her small, delicate Kirlia hands surprisingly steady.

Anya watched her for a moment, a sense of pride in her fellow officer's resourcefulness. Her gaze then drifted to the ancient, petrified wood desk they had salvaged from the archive, now serving as their primary workbench. As an engineer, she couldn't help but analyze its construction.

"This desk is... over-engineered," she rumbled, running a claw along one of the thick, curved legs. "These curves... they're aesthetically pleasing, but they serve no structural purpose. And the weight... it's far heavier than it needs to be, even for petrified wood. It's inefficient design, unless..."

Her voice trailed off. Her eyes followed the strange curve of the leg up to where it joined the underside of the tabletop. In the reinforced section, almost perfectly camouflaged against the dark wood, she saw it: a faint seam, a protrusion that was not part of the natural grain.

"Captain," she said, her voice low and urgent. The others turned their attention as Anya reached under the desk. Her claws found the protrusion. It was smooth and cool to the touch. She pushed.

There was a soft click, the sound of a spring-loaded mechanism releasing. A cylindrical, tube-like contraption, about the length of her forearm, ejected itself halfway from a hidden compartment within the leg of the desk. The outward-facing end was capped with a simple lid.

The team gathered around, their exhaustion forgotten, replaced by a stunned, electric curiosity. "What is that?" Kenji chirped, hopping closer.

Anya carefully pulled the device from its housing. It looked like an oversized, futuristic pen, crafted from the same dark alloy as their new tricorder casing. "It's a precision instrument," she breathed, her voice filled with awe. "A material processor and applicator." She turned to T'Vella. "The book. See if you can find a diagram that matches this."

While T'Vella quickly but carefully flipped through the pages of the enchantment tome, Anya continued her physical inspection. She pointed to a small, hinged door on the side. "This is a material input port. For the Orb Dust." Her eyes then moved to the back of the device, where a sharp, internal needle was seated within a reinforced port. "And this... this is the power source intake. The book mentioned Blast Seeds..."

"Found it," T'Vella announced, turning the heavy book so Anya could see. The diagram was a perfect match, an exploded view of the device with annotations from their ghost.

Anya's eyes widened as she read the notes, her expression shifting from curiosity to pure engineering delight.

"You don't mean..." Zax started.

"Yes," Anya confirmed, a grin spreading across her face as she looked up from the book. "You stab a Blast Seed onto this back needle. The housing is designed to contain the explosive energy, to capture and store it. It's a miniature, single-use power cell."

Her claws traced the two pressure-sensitive buttons in the middle. "This is ingenious. Button one must open a valve, releasing a controlled stream of the contained blast energy through the Orb Dust in the side compartment. It would melt it instantly and spray the molten material out the front needle. It's a spray painter... for enchantable material."

"And the second button?" Eva asked, captivated.

"A bypass," Anya deduced, her gaze flicking between the device and the diagram. "It vents the raw heat from the contained blast directly through the front needle, without mixing it with the dust. For a non-fire type, it's a perfect, controlled heat source for melting dust in a mortar, like I did with my Ember."

She then flipped the device around, noticing two small vents on the back and a port that matched the shape of the front needle. "And this is the safety exhaust. When you're done, you insert the front end into this port. It completes a circuit and safely vents any remaining energy from the blast seed. There's even a sensor here... it detects if an external vent tube is attached. If not, it defaults to a slow, safe release. The ghost who built this... they thought of everything."

This was more than a tool. It was a complete, self-contained, brilliantly designed system. It was a piece of hybrid technology that perfectly fused the raw, explosive power of this world's "magic" with the safety protocols and engineering principles of a Starfleet mind. Their predecessor hadn't just left them a book of theories; he had left them the very workshop needed to put those theories into practice, hidden in plain sight, waiting for someone with the right knowledge to uncover them.

The team stared at the strange, pen-like device in silence.

"So the desk wasn't just a desk," Zax said slowly, reassessing everything. "It was a concealment device. A discharge station and a toolkit, all in one. The ghost left more than notes. They left an entire workshop."

"The level of foresight is... remarkable," T'Vella commented, her eyes scanning the injector. "To anticipate the needs of a potential successor and build the solutions into the very furniture... it is a level of planning that borders on prescient."

Kenji fluttered down from his perch, landing on the desk to get a closer look. "So this thing... it's like a replicator, but for magic stuff?"

"In a manner of speaking," Anya chuckled. "A very, very manual replicator. But yes."

Elara, who had paused her work on the tricorder, looked from the injector back to the heavy desk itself. A new question began to form in her mind. "Captain," she said softly, "this desk... it's so complex. The hidden compartment, the spring mechanism... Do you think... do you think this is the only one?"

The question hung in the air.

"The enchanter in the artificer's district," Elara continued. "The Xatu. They offered enchanting services. They must have their own tools, their own workshop. What if this wasn't a one-of-a-kind prototype? What if the Paradigm Research Group... what if they mass-produced them?"

The implication was staggering. If this desk was a standard piece of equipment, it meant that the ghost's technology had been disseminated, that there might be other, similar workshops hidden away, forgotten. It suggested an infrastructure, a network that had once existed.

"A fascinating, if unsettling, thought," T'Vella mused. "If this technology was once widespread, what happened to it? Why is it now relegated to dusty corners of the archive?"

Eva looked at the injector, then at the book, then at the faces of her crew. The mystery of Alan deepened with every new discovery. They were unearthing more than secrets: a lost age of science and technology, a history that had been deliberately buried or tragically forgotten. And they were right in the middle of it.

Before anyone could answer, a loud, sharp CRACK echoed from Elara's side of the room. Zax spun around, dropping into a defensive stance, while the others jumped in surprise.

Elara looked up from the tricorder, her large eyes wide with alarm. "I'm sorry! I barely touched it!"

She held up the two halves of the old tricorder's casing. A massive fracture had split it clean down the middle of its backside. The internal components were now completely exposed, a delicate web of micro-circuitry and power conduits.

Anya rushed over, her initial shock quickly giving way to an engineer's assessment. She took the two pieces from Elara and examined the break. "Don't worry, Coordinator," she said gently. "It was already structurally compromised from the crash. You just helped it along. The internal components look undamaged." She glanced at the heavy-duty alloy housing waiting on the desk. "Actually, this makes our job easier. Now we don't have to pry it open. We can just slip the guts out and into their new home."

A small, collective sigh of relief went through the team. Losing their last piece of original Starfleet hardware would have been unthinkable. Instead, it was shedding a shell it had outgrown.

The work swallowed the rest of the evening. They were so absorbed in the desk, the injector and the tricorder's transplant that they worked straight through the dinner hour, and it was only when Anya's stomach let out a low, Typhlosion-sized rumble that the team realized they were famished.

"I'll go," Eva said, pushing herself to her paws. "The rest of you stay here and review the schematics. I'll bring back some rations from the mess hall."

She left the quiet of their workshop and entered the main hall of the Guild. It was late now, and the boisterous energy of the dinner rush had faded. Most teams had already retired to their quarters for the night, leaving the hall quiet and echoing. She quickly gathered a bundle of bread rolls and berry skewers, wrapping them in a large leaf.

As she was about to cross the hall to return to her team, the main entrance to the Guild swung open. A chill draft swept through the hall, carrying the scent of the night air. Eva paused in the shadows, her senses on high alert.

A group of Pokémon entered, moving with a grim, silent purpose. They were heavily armed, their gear practical and well-worn. Eva's eyes were immediately drawn to their badges. The gems set within them were not green or bronze, but a brilliant, hard yellow. The Gold-Tier teams. The Guild's elite. They didn't speak, simply gathering in a loose formation, their faces set in hard, determined lines. They were not preparing for a simple dungeon crawl. They were preparing for war.

Eva watched as they filed out into the night, a silent, deadly procession. She knew where they were going. The Murky Wood. The culling had begun.

She returned to her room, the bundle of food feeling heavy and meaningless in her grasp. She entered in silence, placing the food on the floor in the center of the room.

"Captain?" Zax asked, his aura sense immediately picking up on her grim mood. "What is it?"

Eva looked at the faces of her crew, her family. "The Gold teams," she said, her voice low and heavy. "They've been dispatched."

A somber understanding fell over the room. The war was no longer a distant threat; it was happening now, just beyond the safety of Havenwood's walls.

"We're not moving fast enough," Eva said, her voice cutting through the silence. She began to pace the mossy floor, her usual calm replaced by a tense, restless energy. "We've made progress, yes. But the Shadow Corruption is spreading. The Guild is resorting to culling its own. We are in a race, and we are falling behind."

"The Captain is correct," T'Vella agreed, looking up from the book. "Our current rate of data acquisition is inefficient. While the study of this world's 'magic' is fascinating, our primary asset remains our own technology and the knowledge left by our predecessor."

Anya nodded, gesturing to the exposed internals of the old tricorder and the new, sturdy housing sitting on the desk. "This is the key," she said. "We have the new housing. We can start the transfer now. The data dump in that book... it's a direct link to Hexfury's knowledge. We can't afford to wait for the tricorder to charge passively."

She walked over to the satchel and pulled out one of the power crystals they had harvested from the Crystal Caves. It pulsed with a soft, steady light. "This is our solution. As soon as I get these components into the new housing, I can wire the crystal directly to the tricorder's power cell. It will provide a continuous, high-yield power source."

"So that's the plan," Eva declared, her voice firm, her command presence returning in full force. "Our focus shifts now. Anya, you will begin the transfer and integrate the new power source immediately. T'Vella, you will oversee the decryption process once it's online. Zax, Elara, Kenji, we will continue our training, pushing our new abilities to their limits. We will not be caught unprepared again."

She looked at each of them, her gaze intense. "Alan Hexfury left us a message. He told us to create. To shape the world around us. We will honor that directive. We will decode his legacy, we will master this new science, and we will build a weapon that can fight this darkness. We are no longer just survivors. We are the Paradigm Research Group, Mark Two. And our work begins now."

Something in the room steadied. The fear and uncertainty were still there, but now they were tempered with a clear, achievable objective. They were no longer just reacting to their new reality; they were preparing to rewrite it.

"But first," Eva said, her tone softening as she gestured to the bundle of food she'd brought. "We eat. And then we get a full night's rest. We've pushed ourselves to the limit for two days straight. We'll be no good to anyone if we collapse from exhaustion. Tomorrow will be a very busy day. Eat. Then sleep."

The order was a welcome one. They sat together on the mossy floor, the grim reality of the culling hanging over them as they ate.

"Tactically, it's the only move they have," Zax said quietly. "Hold the line. Burn out every breach before it spreads. I'd give the same order." He didn't sound as if he liked himself for saying it.

"It's like something out of an old horror holonovel," Kenji chirped, his usual cheerfulness gone. "A zombie apocalypse. They're not just fighting enemies; they're putting down their own people to stop them from turning."

"The emotional toll on the Gold-Tier teams will be... significant," Elara added quietly, her own empathic senses recoiling at the thought of the cold resolve she had felt from the departing teams.

They finished their meal in a somber silence. The simple act of eating felt like a luxury in the face of the war being waged just outside their walls. They turned in without much talk, the exhaustion of the past few days finally catching up with them. Soon, the room was filled with the soft, steady breathing of five sleeping crew members.

Only Eva remained awake. She lay on her side, her large, red eyes open and staring at the faint glow of the ceiling crystal. The odds were disastrous. They were facing an enemy that could corrupt minds and kill the land itself. Their only hope was a damaged piece of technology and the encrypted logs of a ghost from another universe. The responsibility for her five remaining crew members, the memory of the 135 she had lost... it pressed down on her in the darkness. She watched the slow, rhythmic breathing of her friends, her family. She had to succeed. For them. For all of them. Slowly, reluctantly, her own exhaustion finally won the battle, and the captain's eyes drifted shut.
