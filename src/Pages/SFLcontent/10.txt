The grey thinned, and then all at once it was gone. They burst out of the Murky Wood into sunlight so bright after the gloom that it hurt, and ran on into the long grass, fifty paces, a hundred, until Eva gave the word and they dropped where they stood. Zax went down on one knee facing the way they had come, the phaser still in his paw. Nothing came out after them. The wood sat at the edge of the fields as dark and quiet as it had been that morning, as if none of it had happened.

Anya knelt and laid Kenji in the grass. "He's not breathing," she said. "I can't feel him breathing."

Elara was beside her before anyone else had moved. She laid her small hand flat on Kenji's chest, and the static came apart under her fingers, the way it had in the wood when T'Vella touched her arm. Under it, so faint she had to hold her own breath to find it, something flickered. "He's there," she whispered. "He's still there. It's very small."

Anya already had the tricorder out. It was still locked onto the spike from the wood, every biological sensor switched off, and she swore in Spanish and spent two long seconds clearing the profile before she could sweep it over him. "Heartbeat. Barely. Breathing so shallow the sensor can't decide. And whatever that blast was, it's still in him." She looked up. "His readings are dropping, Captain."

T'Vella crouched on Kenji's other side and studied him without touching. "An Oran Berry will not be enough," she said quietly. "He cannot swallow, and the berries close wounds. This is not a wound."

"The seeds." Anya's head came up. "The glowing ones at the stall. The Kecleon said they bring a fainted Pokémon straight back up."

"We couldn't afford one," said Eva.

"Kenji told us this morning that the Guildmaster had every team stock up on them," T'Vella said. "The Guild will have them."

Eva was already on her feet. "Anya, carry him. Elara, keep your hand on him and tell me the moment that flicker changes." She turned. Zax had not moved from the treeline, and the phaser had not left his paw. "Zax. Satchel."

He held it a moment longer, then made it safe the way he had been drilled to, eyes still on the treeline while his thumb found each control on its own: intensity to zero, aperture to standard, safety on, emitter down, and only then did he slide it into the satchel at Anya's hip.

"Let's move out," Eva said.

They ran the whole way back. The fields that had hummed with calm that morning went by in a blur of gold and green, and the first crystal post on the road flashed amber as they passed it, and nobody looked at it. Anya ran with Kenji cradled against her chest and her flames pressed down to nothing. Elara kept pace at her elbow with one hand on the small, still body, and said nothing the whole way, which Eva decided to take as good news.

They came into the Guild's main hall at a stagger, mud-streaked and scorched, with Kenji limp in Anya's arms, and the hall went quiet around them. Conversations stopped in the middle of a word. Pokémon turned to stare, and a path opened to the reception desk without anyone being asked.

The loud Chatot at the reception desk was in the middle of processing a completed job. "Alright, Team Go-Getters, let's see... six Pecha Berries, as requested by Spinda's Café," he chirped, ticking a box on his scroll with a claw. He peered into the basket held by the nervous-looking trio of rookie Pokémon. "This one's a bit bruised, isn't it? And this one... well, it'll do. Payment is standard rate, minus a small deduction for... rustic quality." He was about to hand over a small pouch of coins when he glanced up, his eyes widening as he saw the battered, grim-faced Team Starfall stagger into the hall. The scroll dropped from his claw. "By the great Dialga! What happened to you lot? An injury! We have an injury!"

"Move, move, this way!" the Chatot squawked, abandoning the rookie team and fluttering anxiously around Team Starfall. He led them away from the gawking crowd, down a short, quiet corridor they hadn't noticed before. "What happened out there? Was it the Murky Wood? I knew that place was bad news!"

"Hostile contact," Eva said, her eyes never leaving Kenji in Anya's arms. "Massive, draconic. Our scout took a direct hit from a blast of black, crackling energy. Whatever it was, it's still in him."

The Chatot let out a worried chirp. "Dark energy... nasty business! Very corrosive to the life-force." He pushed open a simple wooden door onto a small, quiet room that served as the Guild's infirmary. There was nothing in it Eva would have called equipment. Bundles of dried herbs hung from the roots overhead, clay jars of berries lined a shelf grown out of the wall, and a stone basin of clear water stood in the corner beside a stack of folded cloths. Two beds of thick, clean moss took up most of the floor. Two Audino, the Guild's medical staff, looked up from a mortar of something green, their large, sensitive ears twitching at the sudden commotion. "In here!" the Chatot cried. "Lay him on the moss bed. Gently now!"

"Nurse Audie, we have a critical patient!" Chirp squawked, his usual administrative bluster replaced by frantic urgency. "Dark energy exposure!"

The elder of the two Audino was already moving, calm and unhurried in a way that made the whole room feel steadier. She met Anya at the moss bed and bent until the curled feelers at the ends of her ears rested on Kenji's chest. She held them there, listening, then drew them slowly along his wing and up to his throat, and stopped exactly where the blast had struck him. Anya had the tricorder half raised before she caught herself and simply watched. T'Vella's eyes followed the feelers with open interest. A sensor sweep, Eva thought, and a better one than theirs had been. "Life force is fading," Audie said, her voice soft but firm. "His spirit is unraveling. We hold him first." She lifted both hands over him without touching him, and a pink glow gathered between her palms. "Heal Pulse."

The light left her in slow, even waves, like a heartbeat, and sank into him. Elara felt it before she saw it do anything: nothing taken from anyone, only given. Under her hand, the flicker she had guarded all the way from the wood stopped slipping. "It's holding," she whispered. "Captain, it's holding."

"That will keep him a few minutes," Audie said, without looking away from him. "Aden, the emergency kit. Reviver Seed. Now."

The younger Audino, Aden, nodded quickly and rushed to a large wooden chest, his movements efficient but laced with the nervous energy of a trainee. He returned a moment later with a small, gnarled object that pulsed with a faint, golden light, handing it to his superior.

"Alright, stand back, stand back!" Chirp instructed the rest of Team Starfall, trying to maintain some semblance of order.

Nurse Audie took the seed and placed it gently on Kenji's chest. For a moment, nothing happened. Then, the seed erupted in a flash of brilliant, golden-white light, so intense it forced the team to shield their eyes. It wasn't a harsh, energetic light like the phaser beam, but a warm, life-giving radiance that bathed the small Starly in its glow. They watched, stunned into silence, as the light seemed to flow into Kenji's body. Bruises faded, ruffled feathers smoothed themselves out, and the shallow breathing deepened into a steady, healthy rhythm. The light faded, leaving the seed as a small pile of inert dust on his chest.

Kenji's eyes fluttered open. He sat up, looking around in confusion. "Wha... what happened?" he chirped, shaking his head. "I had the strangest dream... I was falling..." He looked down at himself, then at his crewmates' shocked faces. He felt... fine. Completely fine.

Nobody spoke. Anya had the tricorder up before she knew she had raised it, sweeping the little pile of dust where the seed had been. A spent discharge. Organic residue. An energy signature the tricorder logged, flagged and could not classify, and a Starly whose every reading had gone from failing to normal in under ten seconds.

"The law of conservation of energy," T'Vella said quietly. "That much energy came from somewhere. The seed held it, then." Her eyes had not left the dust. "Then where did the seed get it?"

Eva stared at Kenji, preening a perfectly healthy feather, and at the dust on his chest. A seed had just done in seconds what would have taken a full surgical bay and weeks of cellular regeneration therapy on the *Sagittarius*. Every instinct she had wanted to call it magic. She did not let it. It was something they did not understand yet, which was not the same thing, and Starfleet had been built on the difference.

They were still staring at the dust when Guildmaster Ember-Smoke arrived, his slow, heavy steps announcing him. He took in the scene, the recovered Starly, the exhausted team, the dust of the Reviver Seed, and his ancient eyes filled with a deep gravity.

The sight of the Guildmaster, of another leader, was the jolt Eva needed. She felt her composure snap back into place, the awe and confusion pushed down and locked away behind the iron will of a Starfleet commander. She straightened her posture, the weariness in her limbs giving way to a familiar, practiced authority.

"Report, Team Starfall," he said.

Eva stepped forward and gave it the way she would have given it on a bridge. "Guildmaster, we proceeded to the Murky Wood as ordered. The environmental conditions were... anomalous. No ambient sound. No local fauna. Multiple habitats abandoned in haste. Every crystal post along the trail was dark, and from the air our scout saw the treetops at the wood's heart gone grey, as if painted over, spreading outward."

She took a breath, the image of the creature still burning behind her eyes. "The source of the fear is a single, large, draconic entity. Three heads, six wings, dark coloration. Capable of silent, high-speed flight. It fired focused blasts of black energy, what Chirp called dark energy, and Scout Kenji was struck by one during our withdrawal, which necessitated the Reviver Seed. It was extremely aggressive. A territorial species, possibly; we can't rule that out. But my Coordinator believes it was not in its right mind. And my Crafter recorded the energy field it gave off. We know what to look for now."

"The hostile engaged us on our way out," she concluded. "We created a diversion. It appears to have injured a wing in the confusion, and that gave us the time we needed."

It was the truth, viewed from a certain angle. Guardian Zax stood silently, his eyes on the satchel at Anya's hip where the phaser lay, the lie of omission sitting heavy on him.

Coordinator Elara, still shaken from the wood, suddenly flinched. Her head tilted and her eyes went wide, not in fear but in a dazed kind of curiosity. It was happening again, but this was different. Not the chaotic, furious static of the Hydreigon: a clear, complex, deeply somber thought, cutting through the noise of the room. The Guildmaster's. *A Hydreigon... here? And draining the crystals? This is the work of the Shadow Corruption... It's spreading faster than I feared.*

The clarity of it astonished her. She could feel its layers: the surface analysis and, beneath it, a deep and weary dread. He stood so still, so stoic, and his mind was a quiet storm. She said nothing. She had no idea how she could hear him so clearly, and it frightened and fascinated her in equal measure.

The Guildmaster puffed a long, slow ring of smoke. "You have done well, Team Starfall," he said, his voice grim. "You have succeeded in your mission and survived an encounter that would have broken lesser teams. You have also brought me terrible news." He glanced around the infirmary, at the two Audino now tending to a few other minor injuries, and at the open door leading back to the main hall. "But this is not the place for such a discussion. Your report has implications that require privacy. Come with me."

Without another word, he turned and led them out of the infirmary and up the familiar spiraling ramp toward his office at the top of the great tree. He took it slowly, the way he took everything, and the team fell in behind him.

Kenji hopped up the first turn before he remembered he could fly, then fluttered along at Eva's shoulder. "Okay," he said, low. "Somebody tell me what happened. Last thing I remember, I was going up for a better look."

"You got one," Zax said.

"It hit you," Eva said. "You came down out of the canopy and didn't get up."

"Anya carried you the whole way back," Elara said softly. "I kept my hand on you. You were very small for a while." She looked at him, at the bright, ordinary shine of his feathers. "You're not now."

Kenji glanced at Anya, who kept her eyes on the ramp and her flames very low. "Thanks, Chief."

"Don't make me do it again," Anya said.

"The Audino placed a Reviver Seed on your chest," T'Vella said. "It restored you completely. I cannot yet tell you how."

Kenji's feathers puffed out. "The seed? The six-hundred-Poké seed?" He looked from one face to the next. "Do I owe somebody six hundred Poké?"

"Put it on the Guild's tab," Eva said, and it came out lighter than she had expected. "How do you feel?"

He thought about it honestly, which for Kenji took a full turn of the ramp. "Fine. Weirdly fine. Like I slept for a week."

"Good," Eva said. "Welcome back."

Ahead of them, Ember-Smoke had stopped at the top of the ramp, beside his door. He did not turn around. "This is no mere territorial dispute," he said, low and grave. "A great darkness is stirring in this world. And you, it seems, have fallen right into the heart of it." Then he pushed the door open and waited for them to file in.

The heavy wooden door of the Guildmaster's office closed with a soft, definitive thud, sealing them away from the cheerful sounds of the Guild. An expectant silence filled the room, broken only by the quiet hum of the air and the soft puffing of the Torkoal's smoke. Guildmaster Ember-Smoke settled into his usual spot, the late afternoon sun casting long shadows across the room. The relief of Kenji's recovery already felt far away, and something colder had taken its place. They had fled one nightmare only to land in the middle of another.

"Guildmaster," Leader Eva began, her voice steady despite the turmoil in her mind, "you said you had terrible news. What is this darkness you refer to?"

"What you encountered in the Murky Wood," he said, "is a phenomenon we have come to call the 'Shadow Corruption.' For the past several months, we have received reports from all across the continent. Pokémon, normally peaceful, become suddenly and inexplicably violent. Their eyes glow with a malevolent red light, and they are wreathed in a dark, furious energy. They lose all sense of self, attacking anyone and anything on sight. Last month, a Mightyena who has lived his whole life on the outskirts of town, a friend to many, turned on a supply caravan. It took three of our best Pathfinders to subdue him. When the darkness left, he had no memory of the event, only a deep, soul-crushing shame for what he had done."

He paused, letting the chilling anecdote sink in. "We believed it was a temporary madness, an isolated affliction. But the incidents are growing more frequent, more widespread. The Pokémon affected are becoming stronger. Your report of a Hydreigon... that is the most powerful corrupted Pokémon we have yet heard of."

Scholar T'Vella stepped forward, her expression intensely focused. "We observed a secondary phenomenon, Guildmaster. The light crystals in that wood were dark. Not damaged: empty." She glanced at Anya, who nodded. "From what we have observed, a crystal does not make its light. It holds a charge and gives it out slowly, as the ones in our room do. The crystals in the wood held nothing at all. Energy does not simply vanish. If they are empty, it went somewhere, and far faster than they could ever have released it on their own. Something drew it out of them." She paused. "It is not being lost, Guildmaster. It is being taken."

Crafter Anya nodded slowly. "Then the Hydreigon wasn't the cause. It was a symptom." She frowned at the floor, working it through the way she would a fault in a power grid. "Something's tapping into everything in that wood: the crystals, the ground, maybe the Pokémon too. We just saw what it does to one of them."

"Tapping it for what?" Guardian Zax demanded, his voice a low growl of frustration. "And who's on the other end? We can't fight a 'condition,' Guildmaster. We fight enemies. Is this an invading force? A biological agent? A psionic entity? Give me a target to analyze, a command structure to dismantle. Without intelligence, we are simply reacting, and reacting is how you lose a war."

"We do not know," Ember-Smoke admitted. "It is a mystery, a shadow with no discernible source. The corruption has been a creeping plague for many months now, but your arrival... has coincided with a terrifying escalation. It is too portentous to ignore. A silver object, your 'vessel,' tears a hole in the sky. The very same day, the great Sky Guardian, Rayquaza, who has slumbered peacefully for centuries, awakens in a fury. And now you, the survivors of that cataclysm, appear at the site of the most potent display of this dark power we have ever witnessed. Your arrival did not start this fire, Team Starfall, but it has thrown a universe of fuel upon it. Coincidence is a luxury I do not believe we can afford."

As he spoke, Coordinator Elara, who had been standing silently, flinched. The Guildmaster's words were calm, but beneath them, she could hear the turbulent roar of his thoughts, a torrent of fear and uncertainty he kept hidden from his placid exterior. *They see the rage, but they don't see the truth. It isn't anger at all... it's an erasure. A hollowing out of the soul. We can drive them back, but the land remains tainted. The corruption seeps back in. It's an unwinnable war of attrition... It will consume everything...*

Elara gasped, stumbling back as if struck. Her hands flew to her head. "It isn't rage," she whispered, the words tumbling out, colored by the Guildmaster's silent despair. "It's... an erasure... a hollowing out... you... you think it's an unwinnable war..."

Every head in the room snapped towards her. The Guildmaster froze, a wisp of smoke caught in his throat. His calm facade finally broke, replaced by an expression of pure, undisguised shock. His pipe, held loosely in his mouth, clattered to the floor, forgotten.

"How..." he began, his voice a strained whisper that was barely audible. "How could you know my deepest fears?"

Elara trembled, looking at the shocked faces of her crewmates, then at the stunned Guildmaster. "I-I'm sorry," she stammered, tears welling in her eyes. "I heard... I heard you thinking it. I think..."

Nobody moved. Zax's eyes narrowed, already filing it away. Anya opened her mouth on a question and shut it again. T'Vella went perfectly still, looking at Elara as if seeing her for the first time. Elara could hear thoughts. Eva set that aside with everything else that would have to wait.

Guildmaster Ember-Smoke was the first to recover. He slowly bent down, his old joints creaking, and retrieved his pipe from the floor. He did not relight it. He only held it, turning it over and over, and when he looked up he seemed older than he had a moment ago.

"An erasure..." he repeated Elara's borrowed words, his voice heavy. "Yes. That is the truth of it. When we subdue a corrupted Pokémon, *if* the dark energy finally fades... and that is a rare thing... there is a hollowness left behind. A void. Their spirit is diminished. For most, the darkness is total. It consumes them entirely, leaving nothing behind but an empty shell. For the few who we manage to save, it is as if a piece of them has been carved out and replaced by that shadow."

He looked at them, his gaze unflinching. "You deserved the truth. I did not share my deepest fears because hope is a Guild's most valuable resource, and mine is in short supply. It feels like an invasion. A slow, creeping invasion that doesn't conquer bodies, but erases souls."

"Can it be reversed?" Scholar T'Vella asked, her scientific curiosity now tinged with a deep, philosophical horror. "The 'hollowness,' as you call it. Is the damage to the spirit permanent?"

"We don't know," Ember-Smoke confessed. "Our best healers can mend the body, but they cannot mend the soul. The corruption is... insidious. We can cleanse a forest, drive back the enraged Pokémon, but the taint remains in the soil, in the water. Weeks later, it seeps back in. The 'unwinnable war of attrition' your Coordinator heard is our grim reality... We are merely managing a decline."

The Guildmaster's confession settled on Eva like the pressure of a deep ocean. An unwinnable war. A slow decline into darkness. For her entire career, Starfleet's purpose had been to seek out new life, to explore, to understand. This 'Shadow Corruption' was the antithesis of that: it was the destruction of life, the spread of ignorance and rage. It was an enemy Starfleet would have fought without hesitation.

The Prime Directive was a ghost of a memory from a reality they had left behind. Their mission of survival had been achieved, for now. But what was the point of surviving in a world that was slowly being unmade?

But this was not her decision to make alone. Her eyes met T'Vella's first. The Vulcan gave a single, subtle nod, an almost imperceptible shift that was, for her, a resounding declaration: waiting for a world-ending threat to reach them was the least logical course available.

Zax's jaw had set, a grim fire in his eyes; a tactical officer without a war had just been handed one. Anya's flames burned a little hotter. She was looking at a system that was fundamentally broken, and engineers fixed broken systems.

Elara's face was pale from the strain, but there was no fear in her eyes. They burned with a fierce empathy. She had felt this world's despair from the inside, and everything in her wanted to help.

Last, Eva looked at Kenji, perched on Anya's shoulder. The small Starly, who had felt so helpless in the wood, met her gaze without flinching, puffed out his chest and gave his wings a defiant flutter. He was a pilot without a ship, but this was a new course to fly, and he would not be left behind.

It was a unanimous, silent vote. They were all in agreement.

Eva turned back to the Guildmaster with her crew's resolve at her back. She had stopped being a survivor. She was a commander with a mandate.

"Guildmaster," she said, and her voice rang with command again. "Team Starfall is more than another exploration team. We have knowledge and a perspective you do not. Our technology is limited, but our understanding of strategy, energy systems, and xenological threats is not. Survival was our mission until today. It is not enough. We formally offer you our services, as Pathfinders and as a strategic asset in this war."

The Guildmaster looked up, a flicker of surprise in his ancient eyes.

"My Crafter believes something is drawing this land dry," Eva continued. "My Guardian needs an enemy to analyze. My Scholar is driven to understand the physics of this world. My Scout needs a sky to navigate, not one to fall from. And my Coordinator... my new Communications Officer... can give us an advantage no one else possesses. Let us help you. Truly help you."

For the first time since they had met him, a genuine spark of hope appeared in Ember-Smoke's eyes. It was faint, but it was there. He looked from Eva's determined face to the strange, damaged device in Anya's paws, then back again.

"An asset..." he mused. He turned the cold pipe over again, slowly, and for a while that was all he did. "Yes. Perhaps that is what you are."

His ancient eyes went from one of them to the next, and something in them sharpened.

"I have read reports from the Murky Wood for months. Teams come back and tell me it is dark, it is cold, it is wrong. That the crystals have died. Brave Pathfinders, every one, and every one tells me how the place felt." He set the pipe down on the desk between them. "You spoke of energy fields. You told me the crystals were not dead but empty, and that something had taken what was in them. You did not tell me how the place felt. You told me what it was doing."

"Those are not Guild words," he said. "They are not my scholars' words either. Mine speak of spirits and old songs, and they are not wrong to. You speak of the world the way a smith speaks of iron: what it holds, what it will bear, where it will break."

A thin thread of smoke rose from his shell. He watched it climb.

"I am old enough to know when a question will cost more than its answer. A silver vessel fell out of the sky, and you walked out of the forest it fell into, speaking like that." Something close to a smile touched his old face. "Where such words are taught is a question for another day. Today, I would rather have the words."

He looked at Anya and T'Vella, recognizing them as the likely sources of this scientific insight. "Your understanding of energy... Can it be focused? Can you use it to analyze this Shadow Corruption? Find a weakness?"

"We can," Anya confirmed. "But our... instruments... require a significant power source to operate at full capacity."

"Then that is your new directive," the Guildmaster declared, and his voice had found some of its old strength. "Forget berry-picking and escort jobs. I have seen the Expedition Gadgets the great guilds carry, slates of linked orbs that speak across great distances and keep a team's every job in order. Your instrument is some kin to those, I think, and a hungry one. Your first task is to give it what it needs." His gaze settled on Anya. "Then use it to do what none of our scholars can. You called the Hydreigon a symptom. Find me the disease. Find its weakness. Give us a target." A slow breath of smoke left his shell. "Give us hope."

He stood, his posture straighter, his purpose renewed by theirs. "The fate of Havenwood, and perhaps this entire world, may rest on what you discover. Do not fail."
