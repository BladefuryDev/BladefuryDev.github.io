The little lizard with the flame on its tail was the first to move.

"Right," it said, and planted its feet on the scree as if it had decided to stay a while. "Let's start with the easy part. I'm Cinder. I lead this team." It tipped its head toward the green one without looking at it. "That's Leaf." Then, over its shoulder, at the enormous shape behind it: "And the big guy is Rumble."

Rumble inclined his head. It was a slow, careful movement, and he made no sound at all doing it.

"Now you."

Eva had expected the question. She had not expected how badly she wanted to answer it. *Captain Eva Rostova, U.S.S. Sagittarius, Starfleet.* The words were right there, lined up in order the way they had been for her whole career, and they were the one thing she could not say.

So she reached for the other answer, the one she and T'Vella had settled without a word.

"Forgive me," she said slowly. "We were… hurt, in the crash. Our memories are damaged." She let her eyes go down the slope, toward the town, and back. "I find I don't know some very simple things. Could you tell me what you are?" A breath. "And… what are we?"

Cinder stared at her.

Of all the things he had walked out of town that morning prepared to hear, Eva could see, that had not been one of them. His mouth opened and closed. Beside him, Leaf had taken a small scroll and a pencil from the bag at his hip. It was nothing more than a stick of wood with a dark core, its point shaped with a blade rather than any sharpener, and its tip wrapped in a twist of canvas to keep the bag clean. He had unwrapped it, and stopped with the point a finger's width above the page.

"What are you?" Cinder said at last. "You're Pokémon. Of course." He said it slowly and kindly, the way she might have explained to a cadet which end of a phaser to point. Then he looked at her more carefully, at the six of them standing close together on the dark slope, and something in his face shifted from suspicion toward something nearer to pity. He sighed. "All right. I'm a Charmander. Leaf's a Grovyle. Rumble's an Exploud." His eyes went from one of them to the next, taking stock. "And you're… an Absol."

Leaf's pencil stopped.

It was only for a moment. He did not look up from the scroll; he did not need to. Eva felt his attention arrive on her and settle, cool and narrow, the way you feel a draft from a door someone has left open. Then the pencil moved again, and whatever he had thought, he wrote it down.

Cinder had not noticed, or pretended not to. "The tall one's a Gardevoir," he went on. "That's a Lucario. Typhlosion. A Kirlia." His gaze finally reached Zax's shoulder. "And… a Starly."

*Absol.* Eva turned it over once, and kept it.

She watched the others take theirs.

"Lucario," Zax said under his breath, as if testing the heft of a new sidearm.

Anya didn't say *Typhlosion*. She didn't have to. Eva could see her shaping the word without sound, and the corner of her mouth went up, and her fire, which had been banked to nothing, glowed for half a second along her neck.

T'Vella only inclined her head, a quarter of a degree. *Gardevoir.* Filed.

Elara closed her eyes and opened them again and said nothing at all.

And on Zax's shoulder, very quietly, a small round voice said: "*Starly?*"

It was so thoroughly deflated that Rumble made a sound. It was very low, and it came from somewhere deep in the barrel of his body, and Eva realized after a moment that he was trying very hard not to laugh.

"Never seen a team with a lineup like this," Cinder said, and shook his head. It might even have been admiration. "It's… impressive. Weird. But impressive."

He did not let it last.

"So," he said. "Your vessel."

"It was a transport," Eva said. It was the truest small thing she could find.

"A transport from where?" Cinder took a step closer. The light of his tail went over the satchel again, over the scorched fabric and the tarnished buckle, and stayed there a little longer this time. "Because you don't talk like any Pokémon from around here. You're too…" He looked for the word. "Formal. You stand like soldiers. You moved together on that ridge like a Guild team on a job, every one of you knowing where the others were, and there's no team like you on any register I've ever seen." His eyes came back to Eva. "So. Where from?"

"Our point of origin is not relevant to our current predicament," T'Vella said.

Every head on the slope turned to her.

She did not seem to notice. She stood with her hands folded, tall and pale in the moonlight, and spoke in the same even voice she would have used to report a sensor reading. "Furthermore, our recollections of the event are fragmented, in a manner consistent with post-traumatic memory dysfunction. The captain has already told you as much." The smallest pause. "Our leader."

It was a logical evasion, and a truthful one. Their minds were struggling to fit the people they had been to the bodies they were standing in. Eva could have kissed her.

Cinder looked at T'Vella for a long moment. "*Post-traumatic memory dysfunction,*" he repeated.

"Yes."

"Right." He looked at Leaf. Leaf wrote it down.

Zax had not relaxed. If anything he had drawn tighter, every line of him held in check, his weight back and his paw still resting on the flap of the satchel at Anya's shoulder. He did not like this. Eva could read it on him as clearly as if he had said it: three of them asking questions and six of them having to choose, every time, what not to say. On a starship this was called an interrogation, and he had been trained on both sides of the table.

Anya, meanwhile, had stopped being frightened and started being an engineer.

Eva watched it happen. Her eyes had gone to the strap across Cinder's chest, to the small satchel at his hip and the clasp that held it shut. It was metal. Polished, fitted, and very well made. And pinned to the strap above it, catching the light of his tail, was a badge: silver, winged, nearly nine centimeters from wingtip to wingtip, with a gem set in its middle the color of bronze, glowing faintly all on its own. Leaf wore one on his scroll case. Rumble had one clipped to a band around his arm.

Anya's eyes went from one badge to the next. Eva knew that look. It was the look she gave a bulkhead someone else had welded.

"The bad air," Anya said suddenly. "At the crash. What was it like?"

It was not a question Eva would have chosen to ask. But it was a better one than she had expected, because Cinder blinked, and it was Leaf who answered.

He lowered his scroll. "Wrong," he said. His voice was low and dry, and he chose each word as if it cost something. "It's like the air itself is sick. It leaves a taste like static in your mouth and a cold knot down in your gut. Everything in you says *this place is dying, get out*." He looked at them, and his yellow eyes moved over each of them in turn. "And it's on you. All six of you. Faint, but it's there. Like smoke in fur. You can't wash it off."

Nobody moved.

The crew had known, in the way you know a thing in a report. The core had breached. They had walked through the wreck with a radiation clock running and crawled out through the trench with the counter climbing. Eva had told them herself that it would be lethal with long enough exposure. What none of them had thought about was that it might still be *on* them, and that someone might be able to smell it.

Anya's flames dropped to almost nothing. She was staring at Leaf as if he were a piece of equipment she had never seen before, and Eva knew exactly what she was thinking. *They can feel it. Theta radiation, and they can feel it. Like a sensor suite with legs.*

The appendages at the back of Zax's head had gone stiff and still. To him, Eva knew, it meant only one thing: they could be tracked. They had been tracked, all day, across a forest, by something that could follow them by scent.

T'Vella's eyes had narrowed by a fraction. A hypothesis was being built behind them, and then a second to test it against.

Eva asked the only question that mattered.

"Is it hurting you?"

Leaf looked at her.

"The air," Eva said. "What's on us. Is it doing you harm? Any of you? Your town?" She heard her own voice tighten and did not try to stop it. "If it is, we will not come one step closer. We'll go back up that ridge and stay there. I mean that."

There was a short silence on the slope.

"No," Leaf said at last, and some of the narrowness had gone out of his eyes. "It's not like that. It makes your fur stand up. It makes you want to be somewhere else. But it's faint on you. Fainter than it is at the crater, anyway." He tilted his head. "That's a strange first question."

"It's the only one I have," said Eva.

Something on the slope had loosened, a little. It was Elara who opened it the rest of the way.

She had been standing at the back of them all, small and silent, her hands folded together so tightly that her knuckles showed. Now she stepped out. Only a step, onto the loose stone, so that she stood a little in front of the others, in the light of Cinder's tail.

"We mean you no harm," she said.

Her voice was soft. It was not steady. She did not try to make it steady.

"We had a home," she said. "We had a ship, and it was full of people, and they were our friends. There were a hundred and… there were so many of them. And now it's the six of us." She swallowed. "We've been walking for two days. We don't know where we are. We don't know what we are, or what the rules are here, or what's safe to eat. And then we saw your lights." She looked down at the town, at the warm squares of the windows in the dark. "That's all. We saw someone's lights. We're not here to hurt anyone. We just didn't want to be alone out there anymore."

Cinder looked from Elara to Eva and back again.

It was not Eva's careful diplomacy that did it. Eva knew that. She had spent her whole career learning to say true things in the safest possible order, and none of it had moved him an inch. Elara had just said the true thing, with nothing in front of it, and Eva watched the little lizard's shoulders come down.

"Cinder." It came from behind him, so low it was almost under hearing.

Rumble had not moved from his place on the slope. He was still holding his great voice in check, folded down to a murmur so careful it seemed to cost him effort, a sound built for filling halls held to the size of a whisper. "I can hear them," he said. "All of them. Hearts going like rain on a roof." His head tilted, just slightly, toward Zax's shoulder. "The little one was crying, a minute ago."

Kenji went perfectly still.

"They're tired, Cinder," Rumble said. "That's all I hear. They're just tired."

Cinder let out a long breath. His flame dipped and steadied.

"Look," he said, and he said it to Eva, leader to leader. "My job is to keep Havenwood safe. That's it. That's the whole job." He gestured, one small orange hand, at the sky behind them. "And yesterday a giant silver thing comes screaming out of the sky. And a god of the sky comes down and *tears it apart*, right in front of half the town. And then the light. Did you see the light? Everyone saw the light." His voice had gone tight. "And now six Pokémon nobody's ever seen before walk out of the woods from the exact same direction, with that air on them, in the middle of the night." He spread his hands. "You have to see it from my side."

*Havenwood.*

Eva turned the word over. A name for the town, for the lights, for the place below them. A name for somewhere that might, if she got the next few minutes right, take them in.

"We understand," she said. And she did. In his place, with a ship to protect, she would have done exactly the same, and probably worse. "We'll answer what we can, as honestly as we can. But as Elara said, we need shelter. We need help." She held his eyes. "Can you help us?"

Cinder held hers. It went on for a long time, long enough that Eva felt Zax shift beside her and felt him make himself stop. Two leaders on a dark hillside, each of them responsible for people standing at their backs, each trying to read the other and finding, Eva thought, more of themselves there than either expected.

Then the little lizard let out a slow, troubled sigh, and the confident set of his shoulders slipped for just a second, and Eva saw how young he was under it.

"All right," he said. "We'll take you in. You can talk to the Guildmaster."

"Tonight?" said Kenji, before anyone could stop him.

"Tonight," Leaf said. He wrapped the pencil's tip back in its canvas and put it and the scroll away. "He's been up since the light. He hasn't gone to bed. He won't, until somebody tells him what fell."

Cinder looked away for a moment, out past them at the black line of the forest. Something worried moved behind his eyes. "Things have been… strange lately," he said, mostly to himself. "Tense. Even before you." He looked back. "Any new variable, especially one that falls out of the sky and makes a god angry, gets watched. So don't take it personally. But make no mistake." He tapped the badge on his strap, once. "We'll be keeping a close eye on you."

"Understood," Eva said.

Then, at a nod from Cinder, they went down the slope together, three and six.

Cinder led, his tail flame bobbing at the front of the line like a hand lantern. Rumble went last, which put him behind the crew, which Eva suspected was not an accident. He placed each huge foot with great care on the loose stone, and for a creature his size he made almost no noise at all.

The scree gave way to grass, and the grass to a path, worn smooth by a great many feet. The river came up out of the dark, wide and slow and silver. And on the bank, where the path met the water, stood the first of the lamps.

Anya stopped dead in front of it.

It was a post of pale grey wood, about as tall as she was, and set on top of it was a crystal. That was all. A rough, six-sided crystal the length of her forearm, amber all the way through, and glowing. Not burning. There was no flame, no wick, no smoke, no smell of oil. The light simply came out of it, steady as a deck light, and lay on the grass in a soft, even circle.

"No fuel," Anya said, very quietly. She leaned in until her nose was almost touching it. "No wiring. No conduit, no cell, nothing running up the post." Her paw was already drifting toward the satchel at her side. "Eva, this thing is putting out light and I can't see a single thing powering it—"

"There is no heat," T'Vella said. She had come to stand on its other side and was holding one hand a few centimeters from the crystal's face. "I am close enough to feel it, and there is none. It is cold light. Entirely." She looked at it as she might have looked at the first sample from a new world. "I would very much like to know how."

Anya's paw had reached the flap of the satchel.

Eva looked at her.

That was all. She did not say anything; she did not need to. Anya met her eyes, and held them for a moment, and then, with an expression of real physical pain, took her paw away from the satchel and folded her arms.

Up ahead, Cinder had stopped and turned around to see what the holdup was. He looked at the two of them, the Typhlosion and the Gardevoir standing on either side of an ordinary lamp post as if it were a holy relic, and his brow creased.

"What," he said, "don't you have lights where you come from?"

"Not like these," Eva said.

Cinder looked at her a moment longer. Then he shook his head and turned, and went on toward the bridge, and they followed him.

The town was asleep around them. Eva walked at the back of her crew, just ahead of Rumble's careful footsteps, and listened to the river and to the soft sound of their own feet on the path, and turned Cinder's words over in her mind.

*Things have been strange lately.*

Of course they had. She could see it now from his side, the way he had asked her to. A ship had come screaming out of their sky. A god had come down after it. And at sundown, out in the western forest, a light had stood up over the trees like a second sun and gone out. They would have seen it from every hill for a hundred kilometers. Every one of the dark windows she was walking past had looked out at that light yesterday and wondered what it meant.

They were frightened because of the *Sagittarius*. Because of her ship, and what had happened to it. The thought was logical, and it was probably even true, and it did nothing at all to ease the tightness in her chest.

They had arrived in a world that was already afraid. They had frightened it before any of them had set foot in it.

And somewhere ahead, in a tree she could not yet see, an old Pokémon who had not slept since the light was waiting to ask her the same question Cinder had asked on the ridge. The first question anyone on this world had asked them. The only one that mattered, and the one she could not answer.

*Who are you?*

She did not know yet what she was going to tell him.
