The word Pokémon echoed in Eva's mind, a simple label for an impossibly complex situation. She was Captain Eva Rostova of the Federation Starship Sagittarius. She was also, apparently, an Absol. A Pokémon. The two identities warred within her, a dizzying contradiction.

Before her stood the Pathfinder team. The lizard-like one with the flame on its tail stepped forward. "I'm Cinder, team leader," he said, gesturing to his partners. "This is Leaf, and the big guy is Rumble." Eva took a breath, her voice low and hesitant. "Forgive me... our memories are damaged from the crash. Could you tell me what you are? And... what are we?" Cinder gave her a strange look, a mixture of suspicion and pity. It was the weirdest question he'd ever been asked. "What are you? You're Pokémon, of course," he said slowly, as if talking to a child. Seeing that they seemed genuinely lost and not hostile, he sighed and complied. "I'm a Charmander. Leaf here is a Grovyle." The sleek, green creature gave a curt nod, his sharp eyes taking in every detail as he made a quick note on a small scroll. "And Rumble is an Exploud." Cinder then turned his gaze to her crew, his eyes narrowing as he took inventory. “Alright, let's see... an Absol. The tall one is a Gardevoir... that one's a Lucario... a Typhlosion, a Kirlia, and... a Starly.” He shook his head. “Never seen a team with a lineup like this. It's... impressive.” The names and species were now logged, a new, alien identity for each of them.

"Our vessel... it was a transport," Eva finally said, carefully choosing her words.

"A transport from where?" Cinder pressed, taking another step closer. "You don't talk like any Pokémon from around here. You're too... formal. And you work together like a Guild team, but there's no record of you."

"Our point of origin is not relevant to our current predicament," T'Vella stated, her voice a calm, melodic chime. "Furthermore, our recollections of the event are fragmented, consistent with post-traumatic memory dysfunction." It was a logical, truthful evasion. Their minds were indeed struggling to reconcile their past lives with their present reality.

Zax shifted his weight, the movement a subtle threat. He didn't like this. These Pathfinders were asking too many questions. His tactical mind saw them not as rescuers, but as interrogators. He could feel the low energy signature of the phaser against his leg, a cold and inadequate comfort.

Anya, ever the engineer, was studying the Pathfinders' gear. They wore simple leather satchels, but the clasps were made of a strange, polished metal. Practical. Efficient. "The 'bad energy' you felt at the crash site," she rumbled, her curiosity overriding her caution. "What was it like?"

Leaf, the Grovyle, answered, his arms crossed. "It felt wrong," Leaf elaborated, his voice low. "Unnatural. Like the air itself was sick. It leaves a taste like static in your mouth and a cold knot in your gut. It's a feeling that says 'this place is dying'." He looked them over again, his gaze lingering. "And it's all over you six. Faintly, but it's there. Like a scent you can't wash off."

The crew exchanged uneasy glances. The energy of their origin, the theta radiation from the warp core breach, was still clinging to them. They were marked. To Anya, the engineer, the idea that these creatures could feel ionizing radiation was a terrifying and fascinating revelation. It was a biological sensor suite she couldn't begin to comprehend. Zax’s antennae twitched with alarm; being "scented" made them trackable, a critical tactical vulnerability. T'Vella's mind, ever the scientist, immediately began to formulate hypotheses: was it a form of psychic attunement to quantum decay, or a biological organ sensitive to specific particle emissions? The implications were profound.

"We mean you no harm," Elara said suddenly, her voice soft but clear. She took a small step forward, her Kirlia form seeming fragile and sincere. "We are lost, and we are scared. We saw the lights of your town and hoped we could find shelter." The raw, honest emotion in her voice seemed to disarm the Pathfinders more than any of Eva's careful diplomacy.

Cinder looked from Elara's pleading face to Eva's guarded one. He sighed, the flame on his tail flickering. "Look, my job is to ensure the safety of Havenwood. A giant silver object falls from the sky, a god of the sky gets angry, and then a group of unknown, powerful-looking Pokémon show up. You have to see it from my perspective."

Havenwood. A name for their potential sanctuary.

Eva glanced at her crew—at Kenji's trembling form, at Anya's scorched fur, at Zax's guarded stance. Her duty to the Federation was an abstract concept, a universe away. Her duty to them was here and now. "We understand.” She said, her voice firm with a captain's resolve. The Prime Directive was written for Starfleet officers, not for... whatever they were now. They would have to write their own rules. "We will answer what we can. But as our operations officer said, we are in need of shelter. Can you help us?"

Cinder held her gaze for a long moment, a silent negotiation passing between the two leaders. He let out a slow, troubled sigh, his confident demeanor cracking for just a second. "Alright," he said, his voice hesitant. "We'll take you to town. You can speak to the Guildmaster." He looked away for a moment, towards the horizon, a worried look in his eyes. "Things have been... strange lately. Tense. Any new variable, especially one that falls from the sky and makes a god angry, needs to be watched." He looked back at them, his eyes narrowing again. "So make no mistake, we'll be keeping a close eye on your progress."

With an uneasy truce declared, the Pathfinder team turned and began to lead the way down the slope. The crew of the Sagittarius followed, trading the perils of the wilderness for the uncertainties of civilization. 'Strange lately,' Eva thought, her mind latching onto Cinder's words. Of course things are strange. A starship just crashed and its core detonated with the force of a supernova. They must have seen the light for hundreds of miles. They're on edge because of us. The thought, while logical, did little to ease the tension. They were stepping into a world that was already wary, a world they had inadvertently terrified before they had even set foot in it. The Prime Question remained unanswered, hanging over Eva's head like a judgment yet to be passed.
