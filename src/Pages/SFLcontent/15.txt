T'Vella and Anya retrieved the heavy enchantment tome, spreading it open on the floor under the crystal's light. Eva, Zax, and Elara gathered around, looking over their shoulders.

"Right here," Anya rumbled, pointing a claw at the diagram of the mortar and pestle. "This is our next bottleneck. We need a way to grind the orb material into a fine, consistent powder. The book stresses that the particle size is critical for proper fusion."

"A mortar and pestle," Eva mused. "That's a simple tool. Surely we can acquire one."

"The mess hall might have one for grinding spices," Zax suggested. "Though it might not be strong enough for this material."

"The archive is a more logical source," T'Vella noted, not looking up from the book. "Fia mentioned it serves as a repository for old Guild equipment. It is probable that alchemical or older artificer's tools are stored there. We can request access from her in the morning."

They looked again at the schematics they had drawn up for the gloves, the precise circles marking where the lenses of dust would go. They had the orbs. They had a plan to acquire the grinding tools. They had a fire-type to provide the heat for fusion. All the pieces were falling into place. The path from theory to practical application was clear. Satisfied, Eva finally gave the order they were all secretly waiting for.

"Alright," she said, her voice soft but firm, a stark contrast to the sharp commands of the dojo. "We've done enough for one day. Get some rest. All of you."

This time, there were no arguments, only weary nods of agreement. The promise of finally building their own piece of this world's technology was a satisfying thought to end the day on, a tangible goal that made the exhaustion feel earned. One by one, they retreated to their beds. The soft rustle of straw was the only sound for a long moment as each member settled in, their minds still buzzing.

Anya lay on her back, the flames on her neck now just a low, gentle flicker. Her mind wasn't on sleep, but on thermodynamics. Fusing the dust... the book mentioned a specific melting point for the silicate matrix. My flames are hotter, but I'll need precise control. A low, steady burn. I'll need to practice modulating my own core temperature. Another calibration exercise.

Across the room, T'Vella sat in a meditative posture on her bed, her new Scope Lens resting in her lap. She contemplated the physics of it all. Subspace resonance... a material that naturally bridges dimensions. The 'ghost' understood its significance. If we can isolate this property, the applications extend far beyond enchanting simple tools. We could theoretically create our own long-range communication devices, or even a localized sensor grid. The possibilities are... fascinating.

Zax, for his part, lay with his eyes closed, but he wasn't seeing darkness. He was watching the gentle, pulsing blue auras of his sleeping crewmates, a silent, ever-vigilant sentinel. He thought of the Control Glove. An amplifier... if it enhances my control over aura, could I project a shield? Or use it to disrupt an opponent's energy directly? The tactical advantages are significant.

Even Elara, curled up and facing the wall, was not truly at rest. She could feel the quiet, contented thoughts of her team, a soft, warm blanket of shared purpose that was a profound comfort after the chaos of the day. It was so different from the sharp, aggressive thoughts of dungeon Pokémon, or the deep, layered minds of the Guild leaders. This was home.

Eva watched them all, the captain on her watch. The mysteries of the book, the ghost's annotations, the potential of their new abilities... it all swirled in her mind. But beneath it all was a deep, powerful current of pride. They were a Starfleet crew, stranded and transformed, but their core principles—curiosity, ingenuity, discipline, and an unbreakable loyalty to one another—had not just survived; they had flourished. As she finally let her own eyes drift closed, the last thought that crossed her mind was not of her loss, but of her hope. They would not just survive here. They would prevail.
