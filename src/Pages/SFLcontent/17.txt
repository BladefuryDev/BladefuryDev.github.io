Eva looked at them, at her crew, her family. T'Vella, the scientist, ready to codify the impossible. Anya, the engineer, ready to build and repair. Zax, the soldier, ready to fight. Nobody spoke. No one needed to. They all understood the implication, the first true temptation of their newfound power. Starfleet General Order 1. The Prime Directive. Do not interfere in the normal development of any society. It was the highest law, the ethical bedrock upon which their entire civilization was built. This was the power to intentionally and fundamentally alter the course of this world's development, to rewrite its economy, its power structures, its very way of life. The silence was their shared acknowledgement of the terrible and wonderful responsibility that had just fallen upon them. The vertigo remained, but now it had a direction. With each failure, their synergy improved. Anya's hand grew steadier, her movements more precise. Zax learned to feel the rhythm of the Blast Seed's decay, counteracting it with his own aura before it could surge.

Slowly, painstakingly, their skill grew. The simple, straight lines became smooth, elegant curves. The curves became sharp, precise angles. Soon, they were no longer just drawing lines; they were creating patterns, simple geometric shapes that held a faint, latent energy, shimmering on the surface of the stones like constellations on a tiny, gray sky.

"The energy stability is improving by 12% with each successful attempt," T'Vella reported, a hint of approval in her voice. "Your collaborative efficiency is remarkable."

By the time both moons were high in the sky, they had a small collection of stones, each bearing a perfectly executed, un-programmed circuit. They were no longer just a Starfleet crew adapting to a new world; they were becoming artisans, craftsmen learning a new, impossible trade.

Anya held up their latest creation: a stone bearing a complex, interlocking series of circles, the traces so fine and precise they looked as if they had been etched by a laser. "It's not a starship's warp core," she said, a low, satisfied chuckle in her voice. "But it's a start."

Eva smiled, about to commend the team on their progress when she glanced up at the large, glowing light crystal that illuminated their room. Her eyes then drifted to the small, circular window beside it. The sky outside was no longer a deep blue, but a velvety black, dotted with the sharp, unfamiliar pinpricks of alien stars.

"Belay that," she said suddenly, her voice cutting through their focused work. "What time is it?"

The question hung in the air for a moment before a low, rumbling growl echoed through the room. It was Anya's stomach. A second later, it was answered by a chorus of similar, if less dramatic, sounds from the rest of the team.

Kenji's wings drooped. "Are we late for dinner again?"

"It would appear so," T'Vella stated, her usual stoicism doing little to hide the fact that she, too, had been completely absorbed in their work.

"Alright, team," Eva said, shaking her head with a fond smile. "Pack it in. The ghost's work will still be here tomorrow."

With a collective sigh of relief and a few sheepish glances, they began to tidy their workspace. The stones were carefully set aside, the mortar and pestle wiped clean. The tricorder, the all-important satchel, and Zax's Control Glove were left secured on the workbench. One by one, they filed out of their quarters, the promise of a hot meal finally overriding the allure of their revolutionary work.

As they walked, the promise of a meal finally broke their intense focus. "I hope they have those roasted Sitrus slices tonight," Anya said, her stomach punctuating the statement with another low growl. "The Maillard reaction on the sugars... it's ridiculous how good it is."

"A predictable but understandable preference for simple, efficient energy delivery," T'Vella commented, though a thoughtful look crossed her face. "I, however, am partial to the sun-dried Tamato Berry stew. The subtle complexity of the spice blend is quite satisfying."

"You would appreciate the spices, T'Vella," Zax said with a rare hint of amusement. "I've only ever seen a Pecha Berry used as an antidote. From a tactical standpoint, I find myself wondering about its non-medicinal properties. Is it a viable energy source?"

"It's a viable *delicious* source!" Elara chirped, practically bouncing. "They're like... pure sweetness! Like candy, but healthy! We should definitely try one tomorrow!"

Their path took them through the main chamber of the Guild, past the bustling job board where teams gathered to plan their next excursions. Just as they were about to pass it, a loud, attention-grabbing squawk cut through the evening chatter.

"SQUAWK! ATTENTION, GUILD MEMBERS! URGENT ANNOUNCEMENT!"

All heads turned toward Chirp, fluttering dramatically in front of the job board with a bright red piece of paper held in his beak. With a flourish, he pinned it right in the center of the board, the vibrant color a stark contrast to the usual beige and white of the job requests. The room fell into a hushed silence, all eyes fixed on the crimson warning.

Chirp took a deep, theatrical breath. "Team Cinder has failed to report in from their expedition to Rust-Eater Cave! They are now two hours past their scheduled return! An emergency rescue mission has been posted! This is a Bronze-Tier alert!"

A wave of murmurs and worried gasps swept through the hall. A young Sandshrew from another team looked pale, his claws digging into his teammate's arm. Older, more seasoned explorers exchanged grim looks, their expressions tight with shared memories of that dangerous place. Rust-Eater Cave was notoriously difficult, a labyrinth of metal and decay known for its aggressive Steel-type inhabitants. For a veteran team like Cinder's to be overdue was a serious cause for alarm.

Eva's face, usually a mask of calm authority, was now a picture of grim determination. Her eyes, wide and sharp, fixed on the red paper.

"Cinder showed us what it means to be a Pathfinder," Eva stated, her voice sharp and clear, cutting through the rising panic in the hall. "It's time we showed them what it means to be Starfleet. This is most certainly a job for us."

"Zax," she said. "Go back to the quarters. Retrieve the tricorder and the team satchel. We'll need everything."

Zax didn't hesitate. He spun on his heel, the soft pads of his feet making no sound as his powerful legs ate up the distance. He moved with the focused urgency of a tactical officer responding to a red alert. The air rushed past his ears. He burst into their room, his eyes immediately scanning the workbench, cataloging every item. His first priority was the team satchel. The rough texture of the canvas was familiar as he slung it over his shoulder. His paws were a blur, confirming the smooth skins of the berries, the volatile crackle of the Blast Seeds, the cool, solid weight of the Protect Orb. Satisfied, he snatched the tricorder, the faint hum of its power source vibrating in his paw. Finally, the Control Glove. The supple leather and its three cool lenses felt like an extension of his own body. Every piece of their advanced and arcane arsenal was accounted for. Within thirty seconds, he was out the door and sprinting back to the main hall, a silent, efficient blue-and-black streak of pure readiness.

By the time Zax returned, the main hall was a flurry of activity. Several other Bronze-Tier teams were gathering, their leaders talking in low, urgent tones, forming impromptu rescue parties. Zax rejoined his team, handing the tricorder to Anya and the satchel to Eva. He quickly slipped the Control Glove onto his paw, the familiar hum of its attunement a comforting presence.

"Gear secured, Captain," he reported, his voice a low, steady anchor in the surrounding chaos.

"Good," Eva said. She turned and strode purposefully towards the job board, her team falling into a practiced formation behind her. They moved through the crowd with a quiet, disciplined efficiency that parted the other, more panicked teams.

Chirp the Chatot was fluttering anxiously, trying to organize the response. "Alright, settle down, settle down! We need two teams to take the eastern passage, one to scout the main chamber—"

"Chirp," Eva's voice cut through his frantic instructions, calm and authoritative. "Team Starfall is accepting the mission." She reached up and, with a decisive motion, pulled the red paper from the board.

The Chatot stopped mid-squawk, his beak hanging open. The other teams fell silent, all eyes turning to the Absol who had just taken command of the situation.

Chirp blinked, his feathers ruffling in astonishment. He, the Guild's official herald, was used to directing the flow of information, not having it demanded from him. But as he looked at the grim, focused faces of Team Starfall, he saw not arrogance, but a startling level of competence.

"S-squawk! Right! Of course!" he stammered, composing himself with a shake of his colorful plumage. "A briefing! Yes! Team Cinder was on a standard resource-gathering mission in Rust-Eater Cave, lower levels. They were after high-grade metallic scrap, so they were heading deep. Their last check-in was scheduled for after they cleared the fifth basement floor. That was over two hours ago."

He paused, tapping a wing to his chin in thought. "Resistance is primarily Steel-types. Annoying Aron and Lairon, mostly. *Clang, clang, clang!*" he mimicked the sound of metal hitting metal. "But the deeper levels have territorial Aggron, and they are *not* happy to see visitors. There are also reports of Mawile packs using the tight corridors for ambushes. Very nasty, very bitey!"

"Environmental hazards are minimal," he continued, "but be aware of large, magnetized rock formations. They can interfere with the internal compasses of Steel-types, causing disorientation. It's a minor nuisance for most, but can be a real problem for any Steel-types on a team. That's all we know."

Eva processed the information with battlefield clarity. Her stomach, which had been growling moments before, was now a tight knot of adrenaline. Dinner was a forgotten luxury. A rescue mission took precedence over everything.

"Thank you, Chirp. That's all we need," she said, her tone leaving no room for debate. She turned to the other assembled teams. A few of the other team leaders—a gruff Tauros, a sleek Persian—looked annoyed, their own rescue plans momentarily upstaged. "We will take point," Eva continued, her voice projecting a calm authority that silenced their murmurs. "The rest of you, form a secondary support team. You will follow our lead and provide backup if we run into overwhelming resistance. Understood?"

The initial annoyance from the other teams shifted to a grudging respect. This newcomer wasn't just grandstanding; she was organizing. She was thinking tactically. Chops the Machamp, leader of his own respected team, stepped forward with a decisive nod, his four arms crossed. "You heard her," he boomed, his voice resonating through the hall. "Starfall's got the lead. We'll be right behind you. It's the best chance Cinder's got." The other teams, swayed by his confidence and Eva's undeniable command presence, murmured their assent.

Eva gave a sharp nod of acknowledgement. "One final question, Chirp. The location of Rust-Eater Cave?"

"Northwest of here," the Chatot squawked, pointing a wing towards the main exit. "A half-hour's journey past the Whispering Grotto. You can't miss it. The trees around it are all stained with rust."

Eva took a final look at the red briefing paper, then met the eyes of each of her crewmates. T'Vella gave a subtle, logical nod. Anya's flames burned a little hotter with readiness. Zax's posture was a steel spring of potential energy. No words were needed. They were a single unit, ready to act.

"Poyekhali," Eva said, her voice quiet but carrying the unmistakable weight of command. She spun around and headed for the exit at a brisk, determined pace, and her team fell in behind her instantly.

Kenji caught up first, landing on Anya's shoulder. "Captain? What does that mean?"

"'Let's go,'" Eva said, without breaking stride. "Gagarin said it as his rocket left the pad. The first of us to leave the ground."

Kenji was quiet for a moment. When he spoke again, the nervous edge had gone out of his voice. "Good word."

They left the warm light of the Guildhall behind, the worried chatter resuming in their wake, their hunger forgotten, their minds focused on a single, overriding objective: no one gets left behind.
