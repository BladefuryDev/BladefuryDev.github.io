The walk into Havenwood was a sensory overload. The air hummed with life and smelled of damp earth, sweet pollen, and woodsmoke. Pokémon of all shapes and sizes moved through the town's winding paths. A quartet of Bellossom danced in a lamp lit square, their movements generating a soft, harmonious melody. A burly Machoke directed the flow of goods across a tree root bridge, his four arms effortlessly guiding carts pulled by Tauros.

Every head turned as the six strangers passed. Whispers followed them like a breeze. The townsfolk weren't hostile, but they were intensely curious. Elara could feel their collective thoughts—a blend of wonder at their strange forms and suspicion at the faint, alien energy they radiated. Kenji, overwhelmed, fluttered close to Eva's head, his Starly eyes wide. Zax maintained a vigilant perimeter, his gaze sweeping every shadow and doorway, while Anya stared in open fascination at the impossible architecture.

Cinder led them through the heart of the town to its most impressive structure: a colossal, ancient tree whose upper branches seemed to touch the clouds. Carved into its massive trunk was a grand, arched doorway, the symbol of the Pathfinder Guild etched above it. This was the local Guildhall.

Inside, the hall was a bustling hub of activity. Teams of Pokémon were gathered around notice boards, planning expeditions. A Chatot chattered loudly at a reception desk, sorting through scrolls. The air was filled with a sense of purpose and adventure. Cinder led them past the main floor and up a spiraling ramp carved into the tree's interior, arriving at a quiet office at the top.

The room was simple and serene. The walls were lined with shelves holding ancient-looking scrolls and strange geological samples. In the center of the room, basking in a beam of sunlight from a hole in the ceiling, was an ancient Torkoal. His shell was gnarled and moss-covered, and he puffed slow, thoughtful rings of gray smoke from the holes on his back. He radiated an aura of immense calm and profound wisdom.

"Guildmaster," Cinder said, his voice formal and stripped of its usual bravado. He stepped forward with a respectful bow, holding out the small scroll that Leaf had been meticulously writing on. "Team Cinder, reporting as ordered. We investigated the disturbance to the east." He paused, choosing his words carefully. "We found these six near the… site of the impact. They carry the same unnatural energy we detected there. Given the circumstances and the... sensitivity of the location, we felt it prudent to bring them directly to you."

The Torkoal, whose name was Ember-Smoke, turned his head slowly. He took the scroll from Cinder, his ancient, kind eyes scanning the neatly written text with a surprising speed. He absorbed the details of their findings—the crater, the strange metal, the lingering energy, the appearance of these six strangers. After a moment, he nodded, a puff of smoke escaping his nostrils. He then turned his full attention to the newcomers, his gaze lingering for a moment on Eva.

"So, you are the ones who drew the ire of the great Sky Guardian... the one they call Rayquaza," he said, his voice a low, slow rumble, like stones shifting deep underground. It was not an accusation, but a simple statement of fact.

The name struck the crew like a physical blow. Rayquaza. A name for the god-like serpent. A name for the monster that had murdered their crew and destroyed their home. A jolt of pure, cold fury shot through Eva. Zax’s ears flattened against his head, his hands clenching into tight fists as a target was finally given a name. Anya’s neck-flames flared with a sudden, violent intensity, the image of the Sagittarius being torn apart burning in her mind. It was the name of her life's work's destroyer. T'Vella simply filed the data point away, but even her Vulcan composure couldn't completely mask the subtle tightening of her jaw. They had a name for their enemy.

Eva forced her own rage down, burying it beneath layers of command discipline. She stepped forward, inclining her head respectfully. "Guildmaster. I am Eva. This is my crew. We did not intend to cause a disturbance. Our arrival here was... unintentional."

Ember-Smoke puffed another smoke ring, his ancient eyes showing a flicker of something deeper than simple curiosity. "Unintentional arrivals..." he mused, his voice a low rumble. He looked at Cinder's team. "Thank you, Cinder. You have done well. Your report is noted. You are dismissed." Cinder, recognizing the tone of finality, gave another respectful bow. "Yes, Guildmaster." He turned and led his team out of the office, closing the door softly behind them, leaving the survivors alone with the ancient Torkoal. Ember-Smoke's gaze returned to Eva. The old legends stirred in his mind, tales of beings summoned from beyond the sky in times of great crisis. It was a common enough tale for one to appear in a Mystery Dungeon, lost and confused. But an entire group, arriving with a "silver streak" that angered the very heavens... that was different. That was portentous. "Cinder tells me you are lost and have no memory of your home," he said, his voice carefully neutral, hiding the sudden weight of his suspicions. "Amnesia is a common enough affliction for those who wander out of a Mystery Dungeon, but it is... unusual... for an entire group."

He listened, his eyes never leaving Eva's, as she made a critical decision. The Prime Directive was a law for a reality they had left behind. These people deserved some measure of truth. "Our memories are not gone, Guildmaster," she said, her voice quiet but firm. "They are just... difficult to reconcile with our current situation. The land we call home is... very distant. So distant that our only means of travel was a vessel that traverses the sky. It was not meant for this kind of atmosphere. It was damaged upon entry, and... it crashed." The pain of the memory was raw and real, and it colored her voice with a grief that was undeniable. "There were many more of us. We are the only survivors of that crash. The rest of our crew... they did not make it." She omitted the details—Starfleet, the nature of their ship, their original forms—but the core truth was there. They were aliens, castaways, and the last of their kind in this strange new world.

When she finished, the old Torkoal was silent for a long time, puffing his smoke rings. He tilted his head, his gaze distant, as if searching the dusty shelves of his memory. "A vessel that traverses the sky," he rumbled thoughtfully. "From a very distant land, indeed."

The crew exchanged uneasy glances, uncertain of how their strange story was being received. But Ember-Smoke was not merely listening to their story; he was fitting it into a much older, more mythic narrative of his own. The old legends stirred in his mind, tales of beings summoned from beyond the sky in times of great crisis. It was a common enough tale for one to appear in a Mystery Dungeon, lost and confused. But an entire group, arriving with a "silver streak" that angered the very heavens... that was different. That was portentous. He looked at their weary, battered forms, at the unity in the way they stood together, at the mix of fear and defiance in their eyes.

"You are lost. You are homeless. But you are not helpless," the Guildmaster stated, his voice resonating with a deep wisdom. "You possess a unity I have rarely seen, and a fire in your eyes that speaks of great loss, and greater resolve. Such qualities are rare. Havenwood can offer you a place to mend your wounds... and prove your worth."

He paused, letting the offer of salvation sink in. "Charity is a temporary solution. If you wish to make a life here, to earn your keep and find your purpose, there is a path. You can register as a new exploration team. You can put your strength to use for the good of this town. The rage of a Sky Guardian, a falling vessel, and the arrival of a seasoned, capable team... the threads of fate are weaving a complex pattern. It feels like no mere coincidence. The choice," he finished, his ancient eyes twinkling, "is yours."

Eva looked at her crew, a silent question in her eyes. Zax gave a single, sharp nod; a guild meant structure, allies, and a base of operations—a clear tactical advantage over being homeless vagrants. Anya's neck-flames, which had been a low, mournful flicker, burned a little brighter; a guild meant access to resources, tools, and a chance to build, to repair, to do what an engineer does. T'Vella's posture remained serene, but her acceptance was clear; this was the most logical course of action, offering the highest probability of survival and data acquisition. Elara, feeling the sincerity and wisdom radiating from the Guildmaster, offered a small, hopeful smile. Kenji, the small Starly, let out a soft, eager chirp; a team, a purpose—it was a return to the structure and belonging he craved. The Prime Directive was a memory from another life. Their new directive was clear, and they were all in agreement.

"We accept, Guildmaster," Eva said, her voice firm, now backed by the silent, unanimous consent of her crew. "We will be your newest exploration team."
